# Machine-readable literature sources: evidence mapping and trial eligibility

*Research discussion draft, 2026-11-06. Source audit and connection to existing work; no corpus was downloaded, no API was run, and no extraction or coverage result is claimed. This note does not approve a new pipeline design or a change to the research scope.*

## The question behind the source list

There are two related but different tasks. An **evidence gap map** asks which interventions, outcomes, populations, settings, and study designs have been studied under a defined search and inclusion protocol. An **intervention-design search** asks which studies are informative for a particular program choice, including whether treatment versions, delivery conditions, outcomes, and context match. For the RCT transportability idea, there is a third screen: whether assignment, outcomes, waves, and locations can actually be joined for replication and spatial comparison. A large machine-readable literature corpus helps find and describe papers; it does not by itself answer any of the three questions.

The existing [taxonomy pitch](../02_literature-and-evidence-map/Pitch%20-%20Structured%20Literature%20Taxonomy%20and%20Research%20Hub%20Extension.md) seeks multidimensional coding of intervention, outcome, study design, geography, hazards, methods, and datasets. The [Project 01 literature review](../../projects/evidence-map/literature_review.md) is a 15-paper OpenAlex seed, not a systematic census. The current `academic-rag-model/agent/literature` [README](../../../../academic-rag-model/agent/literature/README.md) documents double extraction, verification, synthesis, a query database, an evidence matrix, and an opt-in OpenAlex recall probe. The [2026-11-03 status](../../../../academic-rag-model/docs/status/literature/2026-11-03-subproject-status.md) reports 82 papers with records at that date; the [2026-11-06 trial status](../../../../academic-rag-model/docs/status/literature/2026-11-06-term-selection-trials.md) says term-selection changes were still awaiting rollout and recall was unmeasured. These are dated project records, not a fresh run. The [discovery roadmap](../../../../academic-rag-model/docs/superpowers/specs/literature_taxonomy/2026-10-31-literature-discovery-roadmap.md) already proposes an opt-in external search and review queue after a taxonomy quality gate. A new source strategy belongs in that discussion; it should not be treated as a separate pipeline already approved or built.

**Correction to the proposed architecture:** the most dependable distinction is *labeled study metadata* (3ie), *paper discovery and identifiers* (OpenAlex/S2AG/RePEc), *native full text where available* (PMC/Europe PMC), *parsed full text where available* (S2ORC/CORE), *OA location* (Unpaywall), and *local conversion of remaining PDFs* (GROBID). Coverage, rights, quality, and access differ at every link. We should not promise an end-to-end no-PDF route before measuring those joins on this project's actual papers.

## Connection to the taxonomy and systematic reviews

The promising change is to **start with curated labels and structured text where they exist**, then reserve PDF conversion and manual reading for the records that need them. Existing 3ie vocabulary files are kept locally and gitignored because reuse terms are uncertain, as the [pipeline README](../../../../academic-rag-model/agent/literature/README.md) explains. This public note therefore links to 3ie but does not reproduce its term definitions or paper text. The 3ie labels are useful for comparing the local extractor's intervention and outcome decisions, yet the local project also needs facets 3ie may not code at the required grain: actual study geography versus author affiliation, shock timing, datasets used, randomization unit, and whether an RCT's data can be accessed.

For **gap identification**, an empty taxonomy cell only means “none found and coded in this corpus under this search.” The [PRISMA 2020 guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8005925/) calls for reporting sources, screening, duplicates, unretrievable reports, exclusions, and included studies. The local OpenAlex probe measures coverage against selected top hits, not recall of all eligible studies; the discovery roadmap explicitly leaves a held-out search and review queue to be designed. A 3ie-labeled seed could strengthen the known-positive set, but using only 3ie will inherit its scope and map update cycle. Working-paper, grey-literature, conflict, and disaster coverage need independent search checks.

For **intervention-design relevance**, a matrix count is only a first filter. A candidate card should preserve the exact intervention and comparator, outcome definition and horizon, population, geography, design, crisis state, implementation and delivery conditions, and the passage or curated record supporting each field. An automated label may retrieve a candidate; the researcher's substantive decision is whether that candidate answers the design question. This is where a small, source-traceable synthesis is more useful than counting all papers in a broad “cash transfer × food security” cell.

The two uses can share one paper identity and provenance record while producing different conclusions:

| Output | Defensible claim after review | Claim it cannot make alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence map | Number and distribution of **included** studies under a stated protocol | True absence of research from an incomplete corpus |
| Design-relevance shortlist | Which studies have comparable interventions, populations, outcomes, and settings | That their effects will transport to a new crisis site |
| RCT-data eligibility screen | Which trials appear to offer the assignment, outcome, wave, and location files needed for a specified analysis | That a paper's public full text implies public microdata or joinable coordinates |

## Connection to RCT replication and transportability

The [recent RCT follow-up](../03_rct-transportability/Follow-up%20-%20Data%20Availability%20and%20Transportability%20Critique.md) found more crisis trials than the initial screen, including a published AEA-linked Niger replication package, but left the crucial file-level join unverified. The [Sprint 02 plan](../../projects/empirical_sprints/sprint_02_geospatial_evaluation_matcher.md) targets 15–20 evaluations; that is a proposed corpus size, not an established eligible count. The immediate use of a broader literature corpus is therefore **candidate finding and eligibility triage**, followed by a separate data audit. Counting articles with the right 3ie labels would overstate the number of usable, independent trials.

Three claims need different evidence. **Computational reproduction** asks whether the available data, code, and instructions reproduce a paper's reported estimates. **Independent replication** asks whether a result holds with a new sample, setting, measurement, or analysis; a successful rerun of the author's code does not establish it. **Causal transportability** asks whether a specified effect for a specified intervention and target population can be inferred from source trials under support and effect-modifier assumptions. The [AEA data-and-code policy](https://www.aeaweb.org/journals/data/data-code-policy) explicitly permits documented nonpublic-data exceptions, so even a journal-compliant replication deposit need not release every analysis variable or site coordinate. The [transportability assumptions discussed in the earlier note](../03_rct-transportability/Follow-up%20-%20Data%20Availability%20and%20Transportability%20Critique.md) are not answered by a published replication package.

| --- | --- | --- |

This licenses a **trial candidate count**, not data availability. |
| **2. Reproduction resources** | Data-availability statement, repository DOI or link, software and access notes. | Inspect the repository's manifest, restrictions, code, and needed inputs; a public paper, registry, or deposit landing page alone is insufficient. This licenses a **reproduction-feasibility judgment**, not a reproduced result. |
| **3. Spatial and outcome join** | Reported assignment level, survey waves, location descriptions, and planned geospatial outcomes. | Confirm file contents and stable IDs linking assignment to outcomes and usable site locations at the randomization level; check timing and spatial resolution. This licenses a **spatial-analysis feasibility judgment**, not proxy validity. |
| **4. Spatial signal and proxy validity** | Reported survey outcomes and candidate remotely measured outcomes. | Estimate the randomized assignment effect on a prespecified spatial measure, then compare that effect with the survey effect at a compatible unit and horizon. A detectable geographic change establishes a **spatial treatment effect**; agreement with the survey effect requires a separate, uncertainty-aware validation. A correlation between baseline levels alone cannot validate the effect proxy. |
| **5. Cross-trial comparison and target** | Searchable descriptors of intervention, outcome, population, delivery, conflict or disaster setting, and timing. | Harmonize exact treatment versions and outcome scales; inspect target-context support and plausible unmeasured effect modifiers. The examples show why the gates matter. [Niger's AEA/openICPSR deposit](https://doi.org/10.3886/E192721V1) is a strong *Gate 2 lead* for a village-randomized cash-and-conflict analysis; a search-indexed [file listing](https://www.openicpsr.org/openicpsr/project/192721/version/V1/view?path=%2Fopenicpsr%2F192721%2Ffcr%3Aversions%2FV1%2Fdatasets%2Fraw) names village GPS and shapefile material, but the direct listing could not be opened here, so those file names and their joinability remain **unverified**. Even if village conflict outcomes reproduce, that does not validate night lights as a welfare-effect proxy. The [Somalia grants-versus-monthly-cash trial](https://cepr.org/system/files/publication-files/JA%206728.pdf) offers repeated household outcomes, but every arm received a transfer and assignment was within locations; it cannot answer cash versus no-aid or a village-level satellite contrast by relabeling the arms. The [Sudan digital-cash trial](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/4bc7c996-b094-47d5-a7cf-9114e0b8e0c7/content) is highly relevant to delivery during active conflict, but its public paper does not establish public microdata or precise locations. Its household-level urban assignment is also a poor match for NDVI or night-light treatment contrasts. These are **different failure points**, not one generic “data unavailable” bucket.

For a trial inventory, the useful unit is a **trial or randomized comparison**, with linked records for every article version, registry entry, and deposit. A minimal audit would record: treatment/comparator and estimand; assignment unit; outcome construct, scale, and wave; target population and crisis setting; data/code access terms; location precision and restrictions; and the source supporting each claim. Status should distinguish *not searched*, *not found after a specified search*, *located but restricted*, *files listed but uninspected*, *join checked*, and *effect reproduced*. “No data found” must never silently become “no data exist.” Site coordinates may be withheld for confidentiality; district names cannot be treated as cluster coordinates.

**Low-cost research check, for discussion:** begin with the Bangladesh insurance candidate and Kenya precedent below, the [Niger trial](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aeri.20230069), and a wider search for trials that randomized **villages or other resolvable geographic clusters** between the relevant intervention and a genuine untreated comparison. For each distinct trial, record the 3ie match and labels; intervention and comparator; assignment unit and spillover design; survey outcome and timing; candidate spatial measure and plausible response mechanism; and the repository's access terms for assignment, outcome, and location files. Keep *repository located* separate from *files inspected*; no dataset retrieval is needed for this first screen. A later file-level audit would test the joins. Report duplicates, false candidate labels, restricted locations, and failure points by gate. The denominator is eligible randomized clusters and distinct trials, not indexed papers.

### Priority question: can spatial measures detect village-level treatment effects?

The first test is whether villages randomized to **cash transfers, social protection, or index insurance** show a detectable change in a specified spatial measure relative to eligible villages randomized to a true control. Define the measure, intervention version, follow-up window, geographic unit, and expected direction before looking at the contrast. Estimate the intention-to-treat effect at the randomization level, following the trial's stratification, clustering, and spillover design. A spatial signal is an outcome of treatment in its own right. Calling it a *proxy for welfare* additionally requires comparing its randomized effect with the corresponding survey outcome effect; predicting cross-sectional welfare levels is insufficient. Where control villages are exposed to spillovers, the assignment contrast must be interpreted under that exposure pattern.

This has a strong precedent. [Huang, Hsiang, and Gonzalez-Navarro](https://escholarship.org/content/qt6v9173v3/qt6v9173v3.pdf) used the village-randomized [GiveDirectly Kenya trial](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/unconditional-cash-transfers-increase-general-welfare-and-local-public-finance-kenya) to test high-resolution imagery of buildings. Their analysis used cash exposure in fine grid cells derived from the randomized design; it was not a simple village-level treated-versus-control mean comparison. They found treatment effects on building footprint and tin-roof area, while the night-light effect was indistinguishable from zero. The result makes the choice of *what* is visible from space central: a welfare effect need not change every plausible geographic measure. Roof changes can also be a direct response to program eligibility rules, so an apparently responsive measure need not be a general welfare proxy. Their [public code repository](https://github.com/luna983/beyond-nightlight) states that GiveDirectly field data with sensitive geolocations require IRB approval; the public code alone does not enable an unrestricted exact rerun. This study establishes the basic demonstration already. A useful extension would test prespecified measures across **independent village-randomized trials, intervention types, and settings**, including null results, and assess when the spatial effect aligns with a survey effect.

**Why Bangladesh leads the insurance screen.** The [final paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6472668/) reports that insurance was marketed in 60 of 120 randomized Bogra villages in 2013, while 60 were comparison villages. The policy paid on a dry-spell or area-yield index, **not NDVI**; a dry spell triggered payouts after the 2013 monsoon. The paper finds expanded cultivated area and input spending during the insured monsoon season, but no detectable effect on monsoon rice production or yield. It reports higher rice area, yield, and roughly 8% more rice production in the **following dry-season boro crop**, after payouts. Thus the most plausible first vegetation test is a crop-season signal in 2014, with cultivated area and within-crop greenness considered separately. A higher village-wide mean NDVI is only a hypothesis: insurance was offered to eligible NGO-member farmers, and other farms and vegetation may dilute the signal. The paper's [3ie record](https://gapmaps.3ieimpact.org/kumar-2019-ex-ante-and-ex-post-effects-hybrid-index-insurance%E2%80%A6) identifies an agricultural-insurance RCT, but does not provide the village geography needed for this test. The 2017 working paper and 2019 journal article are versions of **one trial**.

The imagery existed during the trial: [Landsat 8 surface reflectance](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/landsat-8) begins in April 2013 at about 30 m resolution; [MODIS vegetation indices](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/MODIS_061_MOD13Q1) include NDVI and EVI at 250 m every 16 days. These catalog facts establish historical sensor availability, **not usable cloud-free observations over these exact villages**. Landsat's finer pixels look more appropriate for smallholder plots, subject to village or plot locations and imagery quality; MODIS could blur treated farms with other land. [Sentinel-2 starts in 2015](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/sentinel-2/), after this trial, so it cannot supply the study-period comparison. NDVI measures greenness, not yield directly: the first proposed outcome should match a prespecified rice-growing window and a fixed land area, with survey rice area and yield used to judge what the vegetation change means. If a candidate insurance product itself pays on NDVI, the payout index also reflects weather exposure; it cannot be read as a treatment response without separating the two roles.

A read-only CSV check found 1,974 rows, 59 columns, 120 nonempty village IDs, and a treatment indicator constant within each village (998 treated-household rows, 976 comparison-household rows). **Feasibility judgment:** the Bangladesh trial remains a potential replication and NDVI-extension project. The supplied CSV clears the initial *microdata and assignment-ID* access hurdle, but village geography is still the decisive gate for a satellite comparison. A next step is to ask the study authors for a privacy-preserving village-location arrangement (centroids, polygons, or on-site imagery aggregation linked to `vill_b`) and any codebook/analysis code. Site-level geography may be restricted even when de-identified survey data are available. Without that join, the paper is useful for the literature taxonomy and a possible survey-only replication, but cannot anchor the proposed randomized village-level spatial test.

This ranking is **paper-level eligibility, not analysis readiness**. A [49-village Ethiopia trial](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/linking-weather-index-insurance-and-credit-improve-agricultural-productivity-rural) has a true no-insurance group but reports low take-up and no meaningful input-investment gain, making a village-wide vegetation response less promising. A [West Bengal registry record](https://doi.org/10.1257/rct.13781-1.2) is search-indexed as a larger, 300-village insurance-versus-control experiment, but its registration page could not be opened here and outcome results, geography, and data access are **unverified**. Neither replaces the Bangladesh publication as the first evidence-backed NDVI candidate, though a second trial with accessible village geography and microdata could be a stronger *feasible* case.

The [Bangladesh/Nepal forecast-cash study](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sites/default/files/research-paper/Bangladesh_FBF_Working_Paper.pdf) compares an earlier payment with a delayed payment, so its control is temporarily *not yet treated* and the contrast depends on the flood and survey timing. It remains interesting for a waterfall-design question, but it is lower priority for the treated-versus-untreated village test. The Somalia and Sudan trials above randomized households within locations or across aid arms, so their published designs do not yield the proposed village contrast.

The research sequence is therefore: **(1)** identify cluster-randomized trials with treated and untreated villages and plausible spatial measures; **(2)** verify access to assignment, dates, geography, and survey outcomes; **(3)** test the spatial treatment effect; **(4)** test whether that effect tracks the survey effect; **(5)** only then ask whether the relationship holds in another setting or supports a transport claim. Night lights, building features, vegetation, and georeferenced conflict measure different mechanisms and need not move together. A negative spatial result may reveal limited sensitivity of that measure at the village scale, rather than failure of the intervention.
