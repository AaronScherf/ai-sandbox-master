# Literature Gap Memo — verification review

**Review date:** 2026-11-06. **Scope:** Review of the 2026-11-03 Part B memo on `main`, against its sources and work available by this date. This note makes no owner decision and changes neither the memo nor the panel. “Confirmed” below means the linked source supports the stated narrow claim; it does not independently reproduce a paper's estimates. “Unverified” means the specific claim was not established by the material inspected.

## Verdict

The memo is **not ready to serve as a source-verified literature map**. Three of its seven highlighted paper DOIs point to different works, its Sudan IFPRI paper is misclassified as a CGE study, and it omits a close 2024 Somalia district-level study using drought, displacement, prices, and conflict. The local corpus counts reproduce, but absence from that corpus or from a narrow search is not evidence of no prior work. Option B and C remain **plans with access and identification gates**, as the [Part C decision note](Review%20Gate%20-%20Part%20C%20Decision%20Note.md) says.

The Part A audit's price and displacement gaps remain decisive: Somalia's dense local-price window starts just before PRMN ends; Sudan price observations are sparse and selected; the audit describes associations, not reasons. The [cash-transfer RCT follow-up](../../03_rct-transportability/Follow-up%20-%20Data%20Availability%20and%20Transportability%20Critique.md) identifies Niger, Yemen, Philippines, Ethiopia, DRC, Somalia and Sudan crisis evaluations. These do **not** duplicate a Baxnaano × drought × displacement estimand, but they invalidate any suggestion that cash under conflict/disaster is an empty literature. They also do **not** add cash treatment data to this panel. The memo's statement that the **current panel** cannot test transfers remains true; its implication that Option C becomes evaluable by an immediately available district file does not.

One more correction: the shortlist's assertion that VIIRS frees the analysis from Somalia's static population baseline misses a relevant [2025 district-level population reconstruction](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pgph.0005215). Its suitability for this panel is **unverified**, but it deserves comparison before treating lights as a population denominator.

## 4. What changes for Part C?

**A:** Remains a fallback using existing panel variables, with a much narrower novelty claim after Tárraga and earlier PRMN studies. **B:** Still a worthwhile *plan*, but the global price file is the easy part; the baseline share and identification argument are prerequisites. **C:** Remains the strongest thematic Direction I link, but must pass a data-access and treatment-variation check before a causal evaluation can be promised. The owner already chose **B and C as parallel plans only**, Sudan as a portability check, and ACLED plus UCDP as complements. This review neither changes those decisions nor authorizes implementation. No API credentials, paid APIs, data downloads into the panel, or model runs were used.
