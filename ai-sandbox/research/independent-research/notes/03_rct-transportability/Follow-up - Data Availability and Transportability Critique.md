# Follow-up: RCT data availability and transportability critique

*Brainstorm record, 2026-11-06. Three questions discussed with Aaron; conclusions and project-home recommendation remain provisional. This is a source audit, not a dataset inspection or empirical result. No data were downloaded.*

## Question 1 — do the ideas fit together, and is the data sufficient?

**Aaron's prompt:** “First I wanted some frank analysis / critique on whether these ideas make any sense together, and whether there is sufficient panel data available from RCTs to support it.”

**Working answer:** The four steps fit as *successive tests*, not as one estimator. Replicate a trial and its estimand; test whether a spatial measure responds to assignment; separately test whether it predicts the survey effect; only then ask whether a prediction transports to another place. Passing one step does not validate the next. A randomized effect on night lights or NDVI identifies an effect on that measure, not automatically on welfare or a local average treatment effect (LATE). The [surrogate-index paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26463) shows that translating surrogate responses to a later outcome requires an additional surrogacy assumption; satellite measures do not satisfy it by definition. The NBER record identifies the 2019 working paper and a 2024 revision.

A household panel is **not required** merely to estimate an intention-to-treat effect on a measured spatial outcome when assignment, sites, and dates are known. Calibrating a proxy against welfare, studying persistence, and checking cross-site effect prediction require linked outcomes and more than one usable site. Assignment may be at household, block, village, or another level; a village-level raster cannot recover a within-village household contrast. Night lights and NDVI also have different meanings, resolution, and time horizons from survey welfare, mental health, or displacement.

Three assumptions carry the later claims: (1) the proxy is responsive and relevant to the chosen outcome, (2) source and target contexts overlap on effect modifiers, and (3) conditional on measured context, important effect modifiers do not differ silently across sites. Treatment intensity, delivery rail, market access, conflict exposure, and measurement quality could violate the last two. Baseline context variables belong in matching; post-treatment proxy responses need an explicit timing and estimand. [Egger et al. (2022)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.3982/ECTA17945) demonstrate spillovers from cash transfers, so a geographic response can differ from the recipient-level effect.

Hierarchical pooling is a possible later synthesis, as [Meager (2019)](https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/abs/10.1257/app.20170299) demonstrates for seven microcredit experiments, but pooling cannot create comparable treatments, outcomes, or site coordinates. Leave-one-*trial*-out prediction checks external validity; it is not jackknife instrumental variables. [DML](https://academic.oup.com/ectj/article-abstract/21/1/C1/5056401) concerns estimation with complex nuisance functions, not proxy validity. [Bai and Ng (2008)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304407608001085) concerns targeted forecasting predictors, not causal identification of displacement effects. Gemini's “LIML is essential” claim remains **unverified for this design**: a collection of spatial features is not automatically a set of valid instruments. Other unverified references in the earlier Gemini response are not relied on here.

**Provisional signal: yellow.** First establish whether a small set of located RCTs has comparable outcomes and whether a prespecified spatial outcome detects its experimental effects. The number of eligible trials and access to site coordinates are not yet established.

## Question 2 — trials beyond PROGRESA and GiveDirectly, especially crises?

**Aaron's prompt:** “Next I'm curious if there is enough data from RCTs beyond just progresa or other well known interventions, especially in a complex conflict or natural disaster environment.”

**Working answer (expanded after Aaron challenged the first screen):** Yes, substantially more trials and analysis data exist than the initial examples showed. The [AEA-linked Niger replication package](https://doi.org/10.3886/E192721V1) is an especially strong conflict-and-geography lead. A paper's location, a registry ID, public analysis data, and usable cluster coordinates are still four different things. **What the repositories establish:** [3ie's impact evaluation repository](https://3ieimpact.org/evidence-hub/impact-evaluation-repository) indexes published experimental *and quasi-experimental* studies; its quoted 4,600-plus total is dated June 2018 and is **not** an eligible-trial count. Its [earlier humanitarian cash review](https://www.3ieimpact.org/sites/default/files/2019-01/sr28-qa-sr-report-cash-based-approaches.pdf) identified three cluster RCTs and one individual RCT among the eligible older studies, useful as a historical lower bound but not a current census. 3ie's [transparency policy](https://www.3ieimpact.org/sites/default/files/2018-05/3ie-policy-research-transparency-policy%20vApr18.pdf) permits restricted access when confidentiality requires it. [J-PAL](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/evaluation/impact-employment-and-cash-transfers-psychosocial-wellbeing-bangladesh) and [IPA](https://poverty-action.org/assessing-effects-humanitarian-assistance-internally-displaced-households-rct-voucher-program) link several of the evaluations and some public data. Its search-indexed geospatial file names remain **unverified** against a directly opened listing. The [World Bank Bangladesh record](https://microdata.worldbank.org/catalog/8009/study-description) documents repeated surveys and randomization files, and its reproducibility portal lists a package; viewed pages do not establish public village coordinates. A defensible count satisfying **all** criteria—comparable intervention, outcomes/waves, spatial assignment, and accessible joined locations—still requires file-level inspection and remains **unverified**.

**Synthesis so far:** The first screen understated availability. There is at least one especially promising publicly listed village-level conflict/cash replication package (Niger), alongside multiple crisis cluster RCTs and public survey or replication packages. The narrower bottleneck is the *join* among spatial assignment, disclosed site locations, comparable intervention and welfare outcomes, and repeated measurement. The existing compound-shocks panel has no RCT sites or cash-transfer locations, so it cannot fill that gap. The planned 15–20-trial Sprint 2 corpus is a target, not a verified inventory. A later file-level eligibility audit, if authorized, would test the remaining joins; no files were downloaded for this brainstorm.

## Question 3 — how does this map to transfer learning and causal transportability?

**Aaron's prompt:** “Finally I'm curious how well these map to the concept of transfer learning / causal transportability for conflict / disaster settings.”

**Working answer:** The mapping is real, but only after specifying the *effect to transfer*. In prediction language, trial sites are source domains, a proposed program location is the target domain, baseline context variables are features, and site-specific experimental effects are labels. Ordinary **covariate shift** allows the distribution of features to change while the outcome conditional on those features stays fixed; [Sugiyama et al. (2007)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v8/sugiyama07a.html) verify that definition. Here the required stability concerns the **conditional treatment effect**, not merely a prognostic relationship. Calling a method “domain adaptation” does not license a causal claim.

In causal language, [Pearl and Bareinboim's selection diagrams](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/u.osu.edu/dist/7/36891/files/2017/07/PearlBareinboimStatSci2014ExternalValidityDoCalculus-10csxyf.pdf) locate what changes between source and target. If only the distribution of measured baseline modifiers `X` changes, a simple target average effect is `E_target[ tau_source(X) ]`, where `tau_source(X)` is the randomized treatment-control contrast within `X` in the source. [Dahabreh and coauthors](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK596277/bin/72021me150227794_Appendix_L.pdf) spell out the required conditional effect exchangeability and target-to-trial positivity. Target `X` must lie within source support; the same intervention versions and outcome meaning must be maintained. A selection arrow into delivery, markets, treatment response, survey inclusion, or outcome measurement is a warning that reweighting baseline `X` alone may fail.

**Where the analogy breaks in a crisis:** Armed control, telecom access, displacement, market blockades, and aid diversion can alter the *mechanism* of cash delivery and response, not just the mix of baseline covariates. A peaceful rural source may have no support for a target under active siege. Displacement changes who is present and observed; proximity can cause spillovers, as the [Egger trial](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.3982/ECTA17945) illustrates for cash. Distinct trial arms—cash versus no aid, early versus late cash, cash versus food, or grant plus training versus monthly cash—define distinct effects; pooling them as one label would silently change the question. Night lights and NDVI measure geographic signals, while many available crisis RCTs randomized households within a location. For those trials, the geographic signal cannot carry the assigned treatment contrast.

Leave-one-trial-out prediction is a useful **stress test**, not proof of transportability: a held-out crisis may share source support while an intended new war zone does not. A failed check is informative; a successful check supports only the contexts and intervention versions represented. The project's provisional success criterion remains Aaron's “same sign and similar magnitude,” with magnitude assessed on a common outcome scale and uncertainty reported. A good proxy prediction of an outcome level is not necessarily a good prediction of its treatment effect.

## Provisional verdict and cheap go/no-go check

**Yellow, with a stronger reason to investigate than the initial screen suggested.** The AEA Niger package is a concrete village-level conflict/cash lead; Somalia and Sudan have directly relevant trials but public joinable data and spatial assignment are not established. Before any estimator, make a small eligibility table from metadata for Niger, the Bangladesh/Nepal flood trials, Somalia, Sudan, and one more conflict trial: assignment unit, exact treatment contrast, outcome waves, location resolution, file access, and target-context overlap. This is a proposal for later work, not a data run in this brainstorm.

| Signal | Evidence from that cheap screen | Next interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Green | At least two distinct trials of a comparable intervention have accessible assignment/outcome/site links at the randomization level, a common outcome, and plausible overlap with a named target context. | Try a transparent effect table, proxy detection, and held-out-site check before a complex model. |
| Yellow | One trial has the full join, or several have restricted coordinates, incompatible outcomes, or only partial context overlap. | Narrow the estimand or seek access; present bounds and failed joins explicitly. |
| Red | No spatially distinct assignment with joinable outcomes, or the proposed crisis target has no source support. | Do not claim geographic effect prediction; retain the policy discussion or use the RCTs for a different question. |

**Recommended home for now:** a bounded empirical-transportability section of Project 03 / Sprint 2 (RQ 3), not yet a standalone Direction III methods paper. A standalone methods contribution would need multiple usable trials, a clear improvement over a simple baseline predictor, and held-out performance. A later chapter is plausible if those conditions arrive later. Aaron retains the research-question and project-home decision; this recommendation does not decide the compound-shocks options, Sudan scope, or ACLED versus UCDP GED.
