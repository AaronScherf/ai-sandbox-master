# Who would use the predictions? Candidate decision-makers for the use gate (Addendum v3)

*2026-11-17. Extends [Addendum v2](Feasibility%20Addendum%20-%20Trial%20Costs%20and%20Farming%20Base%20-%20v2.md) and [v1](Feasibility%20Addendum%20-%20Fertilizer%20Access%20and%20Trial%20Size%20-%20v1.md). Deliberately long: pare down for the proposal. Everything here is from public web pages, reports and news found 17 November 2026; none is a confirmed commitment, and no organization has been contacted. "Read" means I read the page or report text; "summary" means I only saw a search summary.*

## 1. What a "use" would have to look like

The use gate (Proposal v3) asks: *what response size, at what confidence, would change a purchase, distribution or finance decision?* Four kinds of decision exist in the evidence below:

1. **Where to send discounted fertilizer** (which states, which crops, how much per household).
2. **Which product** (urea, DAP, ammonium sulphate, NPK) and at what rate in a package.
3. **Who gets input finance** (credit lines for sorghum and millet, a stated priority of the Finance Minister in June 2026).
4. **Annual production estimates** (the FAO/WFP assessment missions, which need yield by county).

Our predictions are most useful for 1 and 2 (response and break-even by area), somewhat for 3, and for 4 only as a yield prior. A named decision-maker should be asked, for each: what number would change the decision, how fine must the area be, and by when.

## 2. Sudan

### 2.1 Sustain Sudan (Sustain Africa, hosted by IFDC; CIMMYT implementing) **[strongest candidate]**
- **What it is.** A market-based input program: international suppliers sell to local agro-dealers; discounts rather than free handouts. Roles (summary of [CIMMYT launch post](https://www.cimmyt.org/blogs/building-resilience-in-sudan-sustain-sudan-project-launches-with-strong-partner-commitments/), [IFDC post](https://ifdc.org/2025/08/28/sustain-sudan-partners-chart-path-forward-to-empower-sudanese-farmers/), both read): Sustain Africa project lead; CIMMYT lead implementing partner; Clingendael Institute political and conflict adviser; IFDC host and partner; 249Startups partner; ICRAF hosted the Nairobi inception workshop (August 2025).
- **Targets (2025-2026 seasons).** 260,000 tonnes of discounted fertilizer and inputs; 350,000 farmers directly; two million households; 1.7 million acres (CIMMYT) or 5 million hectares (IFDC; the two pages disagree); yield gain of at least 40% over unfertilized, which is a stated assumption our trials would test. Discounts of at least 30% at retail, with training, digital advisory and a voucher system for smallholders (CIMMYT page; IFDC does not mention vouchers).
- **Progress.** [Port Sudan delivery of 37,000 tonnes of fertilizer and 5,200 tonnes of seed potato, 16 December 2025](https://www.cimmyt.org/news/sustain-africa-supports-fertilizer-deliveries-for-agricultural-recovery-in-sudan/) (read). Distribution started in **Gezira, River Nile, Northern, Kassala, White Nile and Sinnar**: the central irrigated and eastern states, not Kordofan or Darfur. Typical annual fertilizer imports of about 450,000 tonnes reportedly fell to about 50,000 tonnes in 2024. Seeds and crop protection are planned for 2026; Darfur and Kordofan are named for possible seed and crop-protection investment.
- **Funders.** Not named anywhere I read; "donor and private-sector representatives" attended.
- **Fit.** Directly decides what, where and at what rate to distribute fertilizer, and states a yield-gain assumption that is testable. No page mentions soil testing, recommendations or field trials, so a trial network would fill a gap. Staff named on the pages: a project lead at Sustain Africa, a political adviser at Clingendael, a regional director at CIMMYT (names withheld here; on the launch post).
- **Risk.** Unnamed funding; the program's geography (irrigated, central) overlaps little with rainfed smallholder sorghum.

### 2.2 CIMMYT SASAS (USAID-funded, November 2022 to November 2025)
- [Project page](https://www.cimmyt.org/projects/sasas/) (read) lists it as "Active", funder USAID, with soil fertility management and smallholder productivity as the focus; it names no follow-on program. It produced the 2024/25 sorghum assessment and funded IFPRI Working Paper 27 (acknowledgment in the PDF).
- Status after November 2025 and after USAID's changes is unconfirmed. Treat as a source of data and contacts, not a committed user.

### 2.3 FAO Sudan
- **Programs.** In 2025, about 8,393 tonnes of certified seed (mainly sorghum, millet, pigeon pea, cowpea) to about **827,000 farming households**, which FAO says supplied about 22% of national sorghum production (summary of FAO items). For 2026, FAO asks for **$99 million to reach 1.5 million households with livelihood packages including seeds, tools and fertilizers** (summary); a separate FAO item cites a $75 million ask, and a late-September 2026 release cites $44.1 million secured against a $193 million appeal under its 2026-2028 Emergency Response Plan, with only 11% of emergency agriculture requirements funded (summary). A **$616 million 2026-2028 recovery framework was signed with the Minister of Agriculture and Irrigation in July 2026** ([Darfur24](https://darfur24.com/en/2026/07/13/fao-launches-616-million-recovery-plan-for-sudans-agriculture/), summary). The figures cover different scopes and I could not reconcile them.
- **Data role.** Runs the Crop and Food Security Assessment and an agricultural-inputs traders survey (DIEM; not opened, server blocked); cited in IFPRI Working Paper 27 as the source of input-market conditions.
- **Fit.** Decides package composition and where seeds and fertilizer go; its sorghum focus matches ours better than Sustain Sudan's irrigated focus. Funding is the constraint.

### 2.4 Federal Ministry of Agriculture and Irrigation and the Agricultural Bank of Sudan
- Ministry said (June 2026, [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202606030167.html), summary) the Agricultural Bank's plan targets **40% of 25 million feddans** (about 4.2 million of 10.5 million hectares) for the 2026/27 summer season; another report cites a financing portfolio above 3 trillion Sudanese pounds with the Central Bank contributing 20% and commercial banks at least 5% each (summary). Reuters and others describe the bank as hit by the conflict, with a liquidity shortage.
- On 8 June 2026 the Finance Minister met the African Development Bank, the World Food Programme and others to seek **urgent financing for fertilizer, seed, fuel and pesticides for sorghum and millet** ([AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202606090153.html), summary). This is the clearest evidence of a decision about rainfed sorghum inputs.
- **Fit.** The decision is credit allocation by area and crop; it would use response and break-even by state. The Ministry also co-signs FAO's framework. I found nothing from the Agricultural Research Corporation on fertilizer recommendations (a lead, unverified).

### 2.5 NGOs and research partners
- **Mercy Corps** is named in a SASAS farmer testimonial. Its May 2026 country brief ([Prosper Global copy](https://www.prosperglobal.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/from-hormuz-to-hunger-sudan-country-brief.pdf), read) states that **sorghum and millet, the staple rainfed cereals that dominate smallholder production, are significantly less fertilizer-dependent in Sudanese practice, though some semi-mechanized rainfed sorghum systems do use chemical fertilizers**, and that wheat, vegetables, cotton and sesame in irrigated schemes are the fertilizer-dependent crops. It also reports urea at $392.50 a tonne in December 2025 and $725.63 in March 2026 (+85%), and that the 2026 rainy-season planting window (June to September) was ahead of the full price spike.
- **IFPRI Sudan Strategy Support Program** (Working Paper 27, read): research partner and potential co-author; its recommendations include revitalizing input and finance systems, state-tailored interventions (inputs a priority in Aj Jazirah and River Nile; food and security support in the Kordofans) and conflict-sensitive protection measures.
- **African Development Bank and WFP:** in the 8 June financing talks above; potential funders of input finance rather than users of response estimates.

## 3. South Sudan

### 3.1 Ministry of Agriculture and Food Security (MAFS) and the Resilient Agricultural Livelihoods Project (RALP) **[strongest candidate]**
- [RALP page](https://mafs.gov.ss/resilient-agricultural-livelihoods-project-ralp/) (read): World Bank, **$62.5 million, 2021 to 2026**, FAO implementing, target **140,000 farming households**. Locations: Phase 1 Eastern Equatoria (Magwi, Torit), Jonglei (Bor), **Northern Bahr el Ghazal (Aweil East, Aweil South)**, **Western Bahr el Ghazal (Wau, Jur River)**, Upper Nile (Renk); Phase 2 Central Equatoria (Yei), **Western Equatoria (Maridi)**, Upper Nile (Melut), Jonglei (Twic East, Akobo). The ministry page says 15,500 farming households reached with inputs and services and 5,319 farmers with climate-resilient seed; a World Bank status report (summary) says 63,324 farmers received climate-resilient input packages against a target of 50,000. The two counts differ and I did not reconcile them.
- **Fertilizer and trials are not mentioned on the page.** Several RALP counties lie in the states that my model flags as low-risk and with large farming populations (Western Bahr el Ghazal, Northern Bahr el Ghazal, Western Equatoria). The project ends in 2026, so it is a source of partners and sites, not a future user.
- A separate **$30 million World Bank and FAO flood-resilience project** supplying seeds, pesticides, fertilizers and tools is reported in 2024 (summary of [FAO news](https://www.fao.org/newsroom/detail/south-sudan---the-government--world-bank-and-fao-scale-up-actions-to-build-farmers--climate-resilience-in-the-face-of-flooding-and-other-disaster-risks/en)); the government's Doha report mentions a CRAFT project of over $200 million for flood-resilient agri-food systems (summary). MAFS has begun a review of the National Agriculture and Livestock Extension Policy with FAO.

### 3.2 FAO/WFP Crop and Food Security Assessment (CFSAM) Task Force
- The annual assessment ([2025 report, read](https://climis-southsudan.org/uploads/publications/CFSAM_2025.pdf)) estimates county-level cereal area and yield from household visits and crop cuts, and is described as the only independent estimate of national production. It reports no smallholder fertilizer use, Striga damage in the main sorghum areas including Northern Bahr el Ghazal, mechanized sorghum in Northern Bahr el Ghazal and Western Equatoria (mechanized output about 232,000 tonnes), and a 2025 national yield of 1.2 t/ha.
- **Fit.** This is a real user of yield predictions for places it cannot visit, which is our hold-out question applied to South Sudan, not a user of fertilizer-response estimates. It is the most credible South Sudan "decision-maker" for the prediction-accuracy aim.

### 3.3 What is not there
No sorghum fertilizer program, no fertilizer price series and no agro-dealer network in South Sudan turned up. Trade data (summary) show about $1.79 million of nitrogen fertilizer imports in 2023, mainly from Uganda, so a small import channel exists. Treat South Sudan fertilizer use as conditional on a new supply program.

## 4. A first-pass ranking and the questions to ask

| Rank | Decision-maker | Decision our output informs | Why it ranks here | Main weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sustain Sudan (Sustain Africa, IFDC, CIMMYT) | Where, which product, what rate | Stated yield-gain assumption; fertilizer actually arriving; no trials component | Irrigated and central focus; unnamed funder |
| 2 | Ministry of Agriculture and Irrigation, Agricultural Bank, with the AfDB and WFP | Which sorghum and millet areas to finance | Explicit sorghum and millet input financing in June 2026 | Liquidity-constrained bank; decisions are budget-driven |
| 3 | FAO Sudan | Package contents by state | Largest reach (827,000 households), sorghum focus | Funded at 11% of requirements |
| 4 | FAO/WFP CFSAM, South Sudan | County yield estimates | Directly uses predictions for unvisited areas | Uses yield, not fertilizer response |
| 5 | MAFS and RALP | Input packages in Bahr el Ghazal and Equatorias | Project already works in low-risk states | Ends 2026; no fertilizer |
| 6 | CIMMYT SASAS | Data and contacts | Authors of the sorghum assessment | Status after November 2025 unknown |

**Questions for each (use gate):**
1. What per-hectare response at your price would change your choice of product, area or rate?
2. How fine must the estimate be (state, county, scheme)? What interval would be too wide to act on?
3. When must it exist (before the June 2027 planting window, or later)?
4. Would you cite a registered prediction made before the trials, and share your own monitoring data to test it?
5. What are your constraints on where trials can be placed (security, access, data-sharing)?

## 5. What this does to the proposal
- The Sudan use case for fertilizer on **rainfed sorghum** is weaker than for irrigated wheat and vegetables, per the one source that states it (Mercy Corps) and IFPRI's by-crop figure, which omits sorghum. The best-supported claim is for **semi-mechanized rainfed sorghum** (Gedaref, Kassala, Sennar, Blue Nile areas), where chemical fertilizer is already used.
- Sustain Sudan's 40% yield-gain assumption and the Ministry's sorghum financing give two specific, testable decisions. Our Aim 1 predictions could be registered before Sustain Sudan's 2026/27 distribution and checked against its monitoring data.
- A realistic framing is **a prediction and trial service for an input program**, not a stand-alone agronomy study.

## 6. Limits
