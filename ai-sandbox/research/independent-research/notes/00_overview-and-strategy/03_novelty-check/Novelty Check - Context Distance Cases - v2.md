## 3. Strongest Cases for Friday's Peer Talk

### (a) Two Strongest Cases Motivating the Transportability Question (Context Distance Breaks Policy Effects)

1. **Crost, Felter & Johnston (2014, *American Economic Review*): Strategic Insurgent Sabotage in the Philippines**  
   - *Why it is compelling:* It takes the standard "hearts-and-minds" and opportunity-cost theories of development aid (which assume economic assistance reduces violence by building state legitimacy or raising the return to peaceful labor) and shows that in an active insurgency, the intervention produced the exact opposite effect: a substantial, statistically significant **increase in conflict casualties and insurgent attacks**. Because insurgents rationally recognize that successful state projects erode their popular support base, they proactively sabotage projects during preparation. (Note: The Olken comparison comes from footnote 11, p. 22 of the working-paper version, Crost & Johnston 2010 Belfer Center Discussion Paper No. 2010-18, citing Olken 2007, 2010 on local governance and corruption in Indonesia's KDP, not pacification; the paper contrasts directly with counterinsurgency and economic-shock literature). This causally demonstrates that transportability is not just about parameter drift—in conflict settings, armed strategic actors can flip the sign of a treatment effect.
   - *Epistemic strength:* **Measured** via regression discontinuity design across municipalities near the poverty eligibility threshold. Sourced from the working paper version (Crost & Johnston 2010, Belfer DP 2010-18, Tables 2 and 3, pp. 51–52), the RD sample evaluated 160 municipalities across 1,285 municipality-years; the overall program covered 184 municipalities spanning 4,229 villages/barangays, correcting v1's conflation of villages with municipalities. Published in *AER* 104(6): 1833–1856 (`[ABSTRACT-READ]`).

2. **Bold, Kimenyi, Mwabu, Ng'ang'a & Sandefur (2018, *Journal of Public Economics*): State Capacity and Delivery Channel Breakdown**  
   - *Why it is compelling:* It eliminates geographical, cultural, and programmatic distance by holding the country (Kenya), curriculum, and contract specifications identical, testing *only* the delivery channel. When executed by an international NGO (World Vision Kenya), contract teachers significantly raised student learning; when rolled out nationally through the Ministry of Education, the effect was **precisely zero**. The paper investigates the collapse: it was driven by bureaucratic implementation delays (salary delays averaging 3 months in government schools), weaker public monitoring, and political resistance from the national teachers' union (which led government contract teachers to identify with the union and anticipate permanent civil-service absorption, neutralizing dynamic contract incentives). While government hiring saw more local capture/friends of existing teachers, the authors found that patronage did not drive the test score collapse; rather, administrative capacity and political-economy friction dismantled the mechanism. (Quotes removed: the unnumbered author working paper read lacks a formal series and number, and the phrases are not retained from the published abstract).
   - *Epistemic strength:* **Measured** via randomized trial across 192 primary schools (64 NGO, 64 government, 64 control; 188 analyzed at endline), directly comparing NGO vs Government execution arms.

### (b) One Strongest Counter-Case (Evidence DID Transport and Hold Up in Conflict)

* **Bedoya, Coville, Haushofer, Isaqzadeh & Shapiro (2019, World Bank PRWP 8877 / NBER WP 25981): Ultra-Poor Graduation in Balkh, Afghanistan**  
  - *Why it is compelling:* The multi-component "Graduation" model (combining productive livestock assets, consumption cash stipends, life-skills training, and weekly coaching) had established strong proof of concept across six peaceful developing countries (Banerjee et al. 2015, *Science*). Prevailing wisdom asserted that complex, multi-year graduation programs were "too fragile" to transport into active war zones. Testing the model under active Taliban insurgency in northern Afghanistan, this RCT demonstrated that the package produced large, sustained increases in consumption (+30%), asset wealth, and women's empowerment, generating a 26% internal rate of return. It proves that multifaceted "big-push" capital interventions can transport successfully even into severe conflict environments.
  - *Epistemic strength:* **Measured** via randomized controlled trial across 1,219 households in 80 conflict-affected villages.
