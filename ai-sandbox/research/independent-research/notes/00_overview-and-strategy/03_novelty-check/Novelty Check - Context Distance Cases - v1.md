## 3. Strongest Cases for Friday's Peer Talk

### (a) Two Strongest Cases Motivating the Transportability Question (Context Distance Breaks Policy Effects)

1. **Crost, Felter & Johnston (2014, *American Economic Review*): Strategic Insurgent Sabotage in the Philippines**  
   - *Why it is compelling:* It takes the standard "hearts-and-minds" theory of community development (widely proven to reduce petty grievances in peaceful environments like Indonesia) and shows that in an active insurgency, the intervention produced the exact opposite effect: a substantial, statistically significant **increase in conflict casualties and insurgent attacks**. Because insurgents rationally recognize that successful state projects erode their popular support base, they proactively sabotage projects during preparation. This causally demonstrates that transportability is not just about parameter drift—in conflict settings, armed strategic actors can flip the sign of a treatment effect.
   - *Epistemic strength:* **Measured** via regression discontinuity design across 4,000 municipalities with military conflict records.

2. **Bold, Kimenyi, Mwabu, Ng'ang'a & Sandefur (2018, *Journal of Public Economics*): State Capacity and Delivery Channel Breakdown**  
   - *Why it is compelling:* It eliminates geographical, cultural, and programmatic distance by holding the country (Kenya), curriculum, and contract specifications identical, testing *only* the delivery channel. When executed by an international NGO, contract teachers significantly raised student learning; when rolled out nationally through the Ministry of Education, the effect was **precisely zero**. The collapse was driven by bureaucratic resistance, hiring patronage, and unenforced contracts. It proves empirically that transportability fails when an intervention assumes institutional and administrative capacity that the host state does not possess.
   - *Epistemic strength:* **Measured** via randomized trial across 192 schools directly comparing NGO vs Government execution arms.

### (b) One Strongest Counter-Case (Evidence DID Transport and Hold Up in Conflict)

* **Bedoya, Coville, Haushofer, Isaqzadeh & Shapiro (2019, World Bank PRWP 8877 / NBER WP 25981): Ultra-Poor Graduation in Balkh, Afghanistan**  
  - *Why it is compelling:* The multi-component "Graduation" model (combining productive livestock assets, consumption cash stipends, life-skills training, and weekly coaching) had established strong proof of concept across six peaceful developing countries (Banerjee et al. 2015, *Science*). Prevailing wisdom asserted that complex, multi-year graduation programs were "too fragile" to transport into active war zones. Testing the model under active Taliban insurgency in northern Afghanistan, this RCT demonstrated that the package produced large, sustained increases in consumption (+30%), asset wealth, and women's empowerment, generating a 26% internal rate of return. It proves that multifaceted "big-push" capital interventions can transport successfully even into severe conflict environments.
  - *Epistemic strength:* **Measured** via randomized controlled trial across 1,219 households in 80 conflict-affected villages.
