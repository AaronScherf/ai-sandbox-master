# Source verification: humanitarian evidence critique and presentation strategy

*Primary-source web check, 2026-11-09. Companion to the [critique analysis](Humanitarian%20Evidence%20Agenda%20-%20Critique%20Analysis.md) and [updated strategy](Humanitarian%20Evidence%20Agenda%20-%20Updated%20Presentation%20Strategy.md). The [critique transcript](<C:/Users/theaa/ai-sandbox-master/ai-sandbox/research/independent-research/notes/00_overview-and-strategy/humanitarian_evidence_research_transcript.md>) is untracked in the main checkout and was supplied by the user. This audit tests selected claims that materially affect the agenda; it is not an exhaustive systematic review.*

## What survives the check

The critique's **decision-fit argument** is supported in qualified form. Humanitarian decisions span staffing, partnerships, response options, targeting, and information gathering, so an efficacy estimate cannot answer every question. ALNAP's 2019 field study reports that only 3.4% of submitted decisions that sought information mentioned best practice, lessons, or research-efficacy data, and says efficacy data were irrelevant to many relational decisions. The study followed 55 participants, whom its authors explicitly say were **not representative** of all humanitarian decision-makers. It does not establish that academic evidence never matters, that operational constraints always dominate, or that more evidence cannot improve response. Its authors recommend matching analytic, naturalistic, and procedural approaches to the situation. [ALNAP, *Beyond Assumptions* (2019)](https://alnap.cdn.ngo/media/documents/alnap-beyond-assumptions-how-humanitarians-make-operational-decisions.pdf).

Local operational data also have real measurement limits. REACH's JMMI is a genuine public data resource with a global API; its guidance describes market prices and functionality, not a representative household welfare panel. IOM says its DTM methods and periodicity vary by country and may rely on key informants; the public API exposes **non-sensitive aggregate** IDP and household-needs figures. These facts support source-by-source data audits, while correcting the transcript's implication that all data are either unavailable or universally granular. [REACH JMMI guidance (2025)](https://marketsincrises.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/REACH-Joint-Market-Monitoring-Initiative-JMMI-Guidance-Document-V1-2025.pdf); [IOM DTM methods](https://dtm.iom.int/about); [IOM DTM API](https://dtm.iom.int/data-and-analysis/dtm-api).

## Claim-by-claim findings

| Claim in the transcript or original memo | Finding from primary source | Implication for the two drafts |
|---|---|---|
| Campbell and Knox Clarke's *Making Operational Decisions* is an empirical demonstration that decisions are almost exclusively naturalistic. | **Mischaracterized.** The 2018 work is a literature review. Their separate 2019 empirical study observed analytic, naturalistic, and hybrid approaches; about 20.1% of recorded decisions met its naturalistic criteria, 28.6% were hybrid, with other mixed categories. The sample was not representative. [ALNAP 2019 study](https://alnap.cdn.ngo/media/documents/alnap-beyond-assumptions-how-humanitarians-make-operational-decisions.pdf); [ALNAP 2018 listing](https://alnap.cdn.ngo/media/documents/ALNAP-Learning_Links-academic-and-students-Sep2024.pdf). | Retain the timing and decision-fit hypothesis. Remove universal claims about how planners decide. |
| ALNAP's *Insufficient Evidence?* concluded that missing sophisticated causal inference is not a meaningful problem and more evidence does not help. | **Contradicted as attribution.** Knox Clarke and Darcy explicitly argue that good evidence improves effectiveness and accountability, while recognizing multiple evidence types, varying quality thresholds, and uneven uptake. The paper does not establish the value of a transported RCT for every operational choice. [ALNAP 2014 study](https://calpnetwork.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/alnap-insufficient-evidence.pdf). | Present “which evidence helps which decision?” as the open question; do not enlist ALNAP for an anti-evidence conclusion. |
| The Humanitarian Evidence Programme focused only on narrow clinical topics; over 80% of humanitarian systematic reviews concern clinical health. | **Programme characterization contradicted; percentage unverified.** The DFID-funded Oxfam–Feinstein programme commissioned syntheses on shelter, market support, child protection, urban targeting, and health. The “over 80%” figure was not established by the cited programme material. A separate health-evidence review uses an 80% figure for the *quality of studies in a later period*, a different denominator; this may be a conflation, but its origin is not established. [HEP inception report](https://www.gov.uk/research-for-development-outputs/humanitarian-evidence-synthesis-and-communicaton-programme-abridged-inception-report); [market-support synthesis](https://www.gov.uk/research-for-development-outputs/the-influence-of-market-support-interventions-on-household-food-security-an-evidence-synthesis); [Elrha health review](https://www.elrha.org/docs/document/evidence-review-22.10.15.pdf?file_url=document%2F1lmb1q8mvt7kjcc145ts30tn6f%2F5gura4txte9fkw1__nh-gfjlblw%2Foriginal%3Fcontent-type%3Dapplication%2Fpdf&name=evidence-review-22.10.15.pdf). | Drop the percentage and investigate evidence gaps by domain rather than declaring them. |
| JMMI data and a global REACH API are public. | **Supported with conditions.** REACH's 2025 guidance describes released country datasets and a global API. It says the open tier permits 200 calls per IP per month; an institutional tier has higher limits. The same guidance describes market price/functionality observations and asks users to interpret them carefully. [REACH guidance](https://marketsincrises.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/REACH-Joint-Market-Monitoring-Initiative-JMMI-Guidance-Document-V1-2025.pdf). | Identify the API as a concrete candidate, then test relevant country coverage, sampling, update lag, and variables. |
| DTM provides a public API of standardized settlement-level geolocated raw CSVs. | **Overstated.** IOM's current public API is restricted to non-sensitive aggregates at country/Admin 1/Admin 2, with a developer account and subscription key for v3. DTM collection methods and frequency differ by context; key-informant estimates are common in some components. Other DTM products may have different access, so do not infer that no finer data exist. [IOM API](https://dtm.iom.int/data-and-analysis/dtm-api); [IOM methods](https://dtm.iom.int/about). | Avoid treating public DTM API outputs as household or settlement microdata. |
| IATI transactions plus 3W records yield a ready-made planned-versus-delivered dataset. | **Unsupported as stated.** IATI does publish organization-submitted activities, financial transactions, and optional results through a public API. Its datastore represents publishers' data as published, without harmonizing meanings. OCHA describes 3W as a context-specific operational-presence and activity reporting template. Neither source establishes a common project identifier, target/actual definition, or join at district-date level. [IATI datastore](https://reference.iatistandard.org/en/iati-tools-and-resources/iati-datastore/); [IATI FAQ](https://docs.datastore.iatistandard.org/en/latest/faqs/); [OCHA 3W guidance](https://knowledge.base.unocha.org/wiki/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=2397208657&pageVersion=2). | Treat linkage and denominator checks as a prerequisite, not a ready empirical design. |
| JIAF 2 uses a default rule of the max, public microdata across 15+ HRPs, and a mechanical link from sector severity to budget shares. | **Current-method claim misleading; data and budget claims unverified.** An OCHA knowledge-base page describes a max rule in a *PiN aggregation example*, including Syria. The 2024 JIAF 2 manual instead describes multiple steps, joint selection of included sectoral PiNs, evidence convergence, and a multi-partner consensus process for flagged intersectoral severity classifications. It does not verify the transcript's proposed public 15-country microdata corpus or direct formula for budget shares. [OCHA PiN guidance](https://humanitarian.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/imtoolbox/pages/488734850/People%20in%20Need%20PIN%20Process); [OCHA JIAF manuals and tools](https://humanitarian.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/hpc/pages/3993829401/JIAF%2BManuals). | An aggregation/incentives study first needs a version-specific rule and accessible records. The gaming claim remains a hypothesis. |
| Sphere requires rigid fixed bundles such as 15 litres/person/day and gives planners no room to adapt. | **Contradicted.** Sphere's 2018 Handbook says 15 litres is established practice but depends on context and phase; its 2025 contextualisation guide distinguishes enduring standards from phase-specific targets. [Sphere Handbook](https://uat.spherestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/Sphere-Handbook-2018-EN.pdf); [Sphere contextualisation guide](https://www.spherestandards.org/wp-content/uploads/Sphere-Contextualisation-guide-v1.0.pdf). | Keep rationing and equity as possible questions, but do not frame Sphere as a mandated rigid Leontief basket. |
| UNGM makes procurement awards publicly accessible. | **Supported, but incomplete for an exhaustive procurement study.** UNGM says most UN organizations publish awards there, not all. Its statistical dashboards include major contracts with thresholds and withheld supplier names for security/privacy/confidentiality. [UNGM awards help](https://help.ungm.org/hc/en-us/articles/360012822440-How-to-access-contract-awards-on-UNGM); [UNGM supplier-data limitations](https://www.ungm.org/Shared/KnowledgeCenter/Pages/asr_data_supplier). | A procurement paper needs coverage, threshold, commodity, geography, and missing-supplier audits. |
| “GoDARTS” is a named OCHA–Oxford platform whose evaluations calibrate flood-trigger decay curves. | **Name and characterization unverified.** Targeted searches did not locate a primary OCHA or Oxford source for “GoDARTS.” There *is* primary research on anticipatory cash in Bangladesh and a WFP/DIME cluster-randomized evaluation in Nepal, as well as a Bangladesh/Nepal RCT working paper. These show a real evidence line, but do not establish the claimed platform or an operational trigger-calibration project. [Oxford project](https://www.csae.ox.ac.uk/anticipatory-cash-transfers-in-climate-disaster-response); [WFP Nepal evaluation](https://www.wfp.org/publications/nepal-anticipatory-action-impact-evaluation); [NBER working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34414). | Replace the named-platform story with the documented evaluations and ask whether their estimates inform triggers. |
| DRC Foresight is a 1–3-year displacement model covering 25+ countries. | **Supported in outline.** DRC currently describes a national machine-learning model for 27 countries using over 120 indicators, and flags limited ability to forecast unprecedented surges. The transcript's claim that its technical updates integrated specific structural migration mechanisms was not established. [DRC Foresight](https://drc.ngo/en/pages/foresight-displacement-forecasts/); [DRC 2026 report](https://drc.ngo/media/m3zphybx/forecast-report-2026-full-report.pdf). | Cite Foresight as a forecasting comparator, with stated shock limitations; do not present it as a causal transport model. |
| DEEP is a working platform for organizing humanitarian documents. | **Supported, with corrections.** Its own documentation names it the **Data Entry and Exploration Platform**, describes collaborative structuring of qualitative and quantitative material, and presents semi-automated extraction as a longer-term aim. Governance has included UNHCR, OCHA, iMMAP, and other partners; it is not simply an automated NLP classifier or just a passive bibliography. [DEEP documentation](https://docs.thedeep.io/); [DEEP features](https://thedeep.io/content/uploads/2023/04/Features.pdf). | The strategy should compare existing synthesis workflows before claiming a wholly unoccupied niche. |
| Index insurance needs accurate forecasts and evidence that payouts protect welfare. | **The distinction is well grounded; a universal funder threshold is not.** World Bank work distinguishes index–loss mismatch (basis risk) from the downstream welfare effect, and notes that index insurance does not necessarily produce welfare gains. This supports the agenda's separate tests of forecast, trigger, payout, and welfare. The exact predictive or causal standard required by each funder was not verified. [World Bank index-insurance report](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099052825101812947/pdf/P178529-10bf2661-1daf-4e2d-9343-be067fd37699.pdf); [World Bank research synthesis](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/developmenttalk/does-index-insurance-really-work-for-smallholder-farmers-). | Keep funder evidence standards as an empirical governance question. |

## Claims that still need primary-document checks

The transcript also asserts a universal 48–72-hour CERF/flash-appeal turnaround, that more than 80% of humanitarian financing goes to crises older than five years, fixed JMMI inflation or restocking thresholds for automatic modality switching, routine public availability of grant modification requests, and a fully automatic cash disbursement after every anticipatory trigger. The primary sources above do not establish these figures or procedures. The example thresholds in the transcript read as illustrations, not published operational rules. Avoid using them as facts. The same caution applies to causal claims that donor earmarking or “assessment as legitimation” are the **usual** reason plans fail; that requires direct empirical evidence and comparison with other explanations.

## Reading the two drafts after verification

**Critique analysis.** Its main framing—separate immediate feasibility, prolonged adaptation, and advance shock planning—survives. It correctly treats 3W/IATI joins, procurement analyses, JIAF incentives, and insurance thresholds as candidate research questions. The analysis now names the specific external corrections above, especially ALNAP's more nuanced findings and the verified JMMI API. An eventual literature review should distinguish studies of *decision behavior* from studies of *program effectiveness*; the available sources do not prove that operational evidence is always more valuable than causal evidence.

**Updated presentation strategy.** Keeping the 2×2 and 15-slide structure is still defensible as an organizing device. The strategy should describe existing systems more accurately: REACH has a cross-crisis JMMI API, DEEP supports structured collaborative analysis, DRC Foresight forecasts displacement, and anticipatory-action RCTs exist. The proposed research gap is therefore **which evidence can be validated and used for a specified decision**, not the absence of any relevant platforms. Slide 3 should compare actual capabilities; slide 7 should distinguish JMMI from DTM access and resolution; slide 11 should guard against unidentifiable transported effects; slides 14–15 can use the documented anticipatory-cash evaluations as examples without promising a live causal simulator.
