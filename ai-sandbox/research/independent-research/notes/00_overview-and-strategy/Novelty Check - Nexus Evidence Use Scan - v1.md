## 2. Quantitative Summary

### (a) Breakdown by Hypothesis Fit
- **Hypothesis A** (*Little demand beyond operational logistics*): **0 primary** (though noted as an operational reality in Campbell & Knox Clarke 2019, operational logistics is not the sole demand).
- **Hypothesis B** (*Abundant evidence supplied, but not used*): **3 secondary / partial** (Campbell & Knox Clarke 2019; Maxwell & Stobaugh 2012; CALP Network 2023). High-level research, evaluations, and needs assessments exist in volume, but are bypassed because organizational mandates, donor earmarks, and operational habits dictate choices.
- **Hypothesis C** (*Evidence used broadly and effectively*): **0**. None of the 16 sources found evidence being used broadly, systematically, or effectively to drive programme design or adaptation in conflict or protracted crises.
- **Hypothesis D** (*Planners make do with what they have, and say they would do better with evidence that is more relevant to their context, more specific [treatment effects, comparisons of intervention types], or less technical and quicker to use*): **14 primary**. This is the dominant finding across project appraisal documents (World Bank, FCDO), independent institutional reviews (IEG, BASIC Research, IAHE), and practitioner surveys (Elrha, ALNAP, CALP, Tufts).

### (b) Breakdown by Setting Type
- **Conflict or Complex Crisis (FCV / Insecure / Protracted Setting):** **12 sources** (Somalia, Yemen, South Sudan, Eastern DRC, Borno State/Northeast Nigeria, and global FCAS/refugee contexts).
- **Global Humanitarian / Cross-Setting (covering acute emergencies and protracted displacement):** **4 sources** (CALP 2023, Elrha 2022, ALNAP 2014, ALNAP 2019).
- **Disaster, Drought, or Peaceful Poor Country Only:** **0 sources** (the scan strictly isolated conflict, protracted crisis, and nexus programming contexts).

   > *"the format, length, timing and accessibility of evidence outputs are often poorly aligned with the realities of decision-making contexts"*

## 4. What the Scan Could Not Cover

1. **Non-English Project Appraisal Documentation:** Did not review French-, Arabic-, or Spanish-language appraisal reports from regional multilateral banks (such as the African Development Bank, Islamic Development Bank, or AECID).
2. **Confidential and Non-Public Internal Donor Annexes:** Internal risk matrices, confidential procurement memoranda, and unredacted fiduciary reviews from donors (FCDO, USAID, ECHO) that are not published on public transparency portals (DevTracker, IATI, or World Bank Documents).
3. **Real-Time Live Coordination Dynamics:** Live decision-making during active cluster coordination meetings (e.g., National Cash Working Groups, Humanitarian Country Teams, or Inter-Cluster Coordination Groups during an active sudden shock).
4. **Empirical Outcomes of Evidence-Tailoring Interventions:** The literature documents that planners *demand* tailored and "good enough" evidence; it does not yet contain rigorous empirical evaluations comparing whether programmes designed with decision-tailored evidence achieve statistically superior welfare or resilience outcomes compared to those designed via status-quo heuristics.
