## Slide

**Title:** A test case for "how far does evidence travel?"

- Two cash-transfer studies in Kenya report very different spillover effects on the local economy:
  - A randomized trial of one-time transfers (Egger et al.): general-equilibrium multiplier estimated with village-level randomization and spatial instruments.
  - The evaluation of the Hunger Safety Net Programme Phase 2 (OPM): local-economy model (LEWIE) of income per shilling transferred, in four northern counties.
- **We cannot yet say why they differ.** The two measures are defined differently and are not directly comparable; the programmes differ in design, payment pattern and setting.
- **Both are on the replication list**: the Egger package is public, and the HSNP evaluation data are in the World Bank microdata catalog. Start by harmonizing the definitions, then ask whether a measurable context factor (market integration, remoteness, drought) explains the gap.
- Why it matters for the agenda: a within-country case where the same kind of intervention produced different spillover results is a concrete test of whether we can say **beforehand** how far a finding travels.
- Counter-case: Bedoya et al. (graduation programme, Afghanistan) was pre-registered and worked in a conflict setting where earlier evidence came from stable ones; no replication data were found.
