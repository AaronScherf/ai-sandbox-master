# Abstract: finding, assessing and adapting external evidence for decisions in prolonged crises (v1)

*Draft 2026-11-12 for a faculty conversation. No figures cited; the literature scan behind it is not systematic. Review documents v1 to v8 hold the reasoning.*

## Abstract

Programmes in prolonged crises and in the transition between humanitarian relief and development are designed under constraints set by donors, headquarters, budgets and norms, and by planners who must choose among options with imperfect evidence. The literature on evidence use in this setting is mixed. Some interventions, such as cash transfers, are well studied yet unevenly adopted; others have little comparative evidence; and the same intervention can produce very different results as conditions change, including conflict, market access and delivery capacity. Designing from the available evidence is therefore not a matter of copying what worked elsewhere. This project asks three linked questions. **First (RQ1),** how much discretion do planners actually have on a given design decision, and how do they value the evidence available to them? The aim is to identify where new evidence could change a choice, and where constraints, including policies set by the organization's own central office, make it unusable, so that research effort and evidence-generation funds go where they can be used. **Second (RQ2),** what is the decision space once constraints are mapped, and how can planners and researchers represent it in a tractable form, through participatory causal maps, scenarios and per-scenario log-frames? **Third (RQ3),** can evidence be conditioned on context, and comparable settings identified (including across countries, for example insecure regions that resemble each other more than their own national averages), to improve the chance that an intervention succeeds despite the crisis, and can the resulting predictions be tested prospectively? The contribution is a way to measure discretion and the value of evidence for specific decisions, a method for judging how far a finding travels and for which decisions, and guidance for the funders who decide what evidence to generate next.

## Short version (three sentences)

Planners in prolonged crises choose designs under real constraints, and the evidence they can draw on is plentiful for some interventions, sparse for others, and often poorly matched to their context. The project measures planner discretion and the value planners place on evidence (RQ1), maps the decision space (RQ2), and builds diagnostics for adapting evidence across contexts, including across similar crisis settings (RQ3). It aims to show where new or better-matched evidence would change decisions, and where it would not, so that research and programme resources are used where they matter.
