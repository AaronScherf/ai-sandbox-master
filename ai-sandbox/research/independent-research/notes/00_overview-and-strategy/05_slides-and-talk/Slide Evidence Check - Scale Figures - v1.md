## 2. Claim 1: World Bank Extreme Poverty in Fragile Situations

### Slide Claim
> "a growing share of the world's extreme poor lives in fragile and conflict-affected situations; the World Bank's 2025 projection is roughly 435 million people, nearly 60 percent of the extreme poor, by 2030."

### Primary Source Verification
- **Primary Source:** World Bank, *Global Economic Prospects, June 2025*, Chapter 4: "Fragile and Conflict-Affected Situations: Intertwined Crises, Multiple Vulnerabilities" `[FULL-TEXT-READ]`.
  - **Issuing Body:** World Bank Group (Development Prospects Group).
  - **Date:** 27 June 2025.
  - **Primary URLs:** 
    - Report landing page: [World Bank Publication Page](https://www.worldbank.org/en/research/publication/fragile-and-conflict-affected-situations-vulnerabilities)
    - Direct PDF: [OpenKnowledge Repository Bitstream](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/35bb4b31-e9b0-4a1e-8c6c-df4336558673/content)
    - Press Release: [World Bank Press Release (June 27, 2025)](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/06/27/fragile-and-conflict-affected-situations-intertwined-crises-multiple-vulnerabilities)
- **Location in Source:** Chapter 4, page 24 (report pagination p. 14); Executive Summary p. 11; Figure 4.8 and Note p. 25.
- **Short Quotation (<25 words):**
  > "Projections suggest that by 2030, more than 435 million people will be living in extreme poverty in FCS economies, accounting for almost 60 percent of the world’s extreme poor" (p. 24).

### Key Technical Parameters Recorded
1. **Exact Figures & Baseline:**
   - **2030 Projection:** "more than 435 million people", "accounting for almost 60 percent of the world's extreme poor" (p. 24). (Executive summary p. 11 states: "nearly 60 percent").
   - **2025 Baseline:** In 2025, an estimated **421 million people** in FCS economies live in extreme poverty (almost 40% of the FCS population), having in 2024 for the first time surpassed the number of extreme poor in all other developing economies combined, despite FCS representing under 15% of the world population (p. 24).
2. **Year the Projection Refers To:**
   - Projection target year is **2030** (projection published in June 2025).
3. **International Poverty Line and PPP Year:**
   - **Threshold:** Living on less than **$3.00 per day** in **2021 purchasing power parity (PPP)** (Figure 4.8 Note, p. 25; p. 11; p. 18).
   - *Technical context:* This reflects the World Bank's adoption of the 2021 International Comparison Program (ICP) PPP benchmarks, which updated the international extreme poverty line from $2.15/day (2017 PPP) to $3.00/day (2021 PPP).
4. **FCS Definition and Fiscal Year:**
   - Uses the World Bank's **Fiscal Year 2025 (FY25) FCS list** (Chapter 4, p. 13, Footnotes 1 & 2).
   - Applied retroactively across time series for consistency. Covers **39 economies** (21 in active conflict, 18 characterized by institutional and social fragility; p. 18, 24).
5. **Sensitivity to the Poverty Line:**
   - *Within the report:* Chapter 4 does **not** report an explicit sensitivity analysis of the 2030 ~60% projection across alternative higher poverty lines; all global aggregate figures in this chapter are benchmarked exclusively to the $3.00/day (2021 PPP) extreme poverty line. (Case studies in the chapter examine $4.20/day for lower-middle-income countries).
   - *Broader context:* Earlier World Bank research (Corral et al. 2020) and independent analyses note that the FCS concentration is highly sensitive to the poverty line: at higher thresholds (e.g. $3.65 or $6.85), large populous lower-middle-income countries dominate the headcount, reducing the FCS share substantially.
- **Verdict:** **SUPPORTED [FULL-TEXT-READ]**.
- **Recommended Exact Slide Wording:**
  > "By 2030, over 435 million people—almost 60% of the world's extreme poor (<$3.00/day, 2021 PPP)—are projected to live in fragile and conflict-affected situations (World Bank, June 2025; FY25 FCS list)."

## 3. Claim 2: UNHCR Protracted Refugee Situations

### Slide Claim
> "about seven in ten refugees are in protracted situations (UNHCR Global Trends, 2025 data)."

### Primary Source Verification
- **Primary Source:** UNHCR, *Global Trends: Forced Displacement in 2025* `[FULL-TEXT-READ]`.
  - **Issuing Body:** United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees (UNHCR).
  - **Date:** 11 June 2026.
  - **Primary URLs:**
    - ReliefWeb Full Report & Portal: [ReliefWeb Global Trends 2025](https://reliefweb.int/report/world/global-trends-forced-displacement-2025-enar)
    - Direct PDF: [ReliefWeb Attachments PDF (4.31 MB)](https://reliefweb.int/attachments/c8e72ab9-b48a-4254-a466-35c43d68a1f4/global-trends-2025-embargoed-report.pdf)
    - Direct UNHCR site: `https://www.unhcr.org/sites/default/files/2026-06/unhcr-global-trends-report-embargoed-hc-briefing.pdf` `[BLOCKED-403]` (Cloudflare bot challenge).
- **Location in Source:** Infographic p. 3; Chapter 1, "Protracted refugee situations", p. 10; Figure 1, p. 10.
- **Short Quotation (<25 words):**
  > "Almost seven in ten refugees globally are in protracted situations, based on UNHCR’s definition of protracted refugee situations" (p. 10).

### Key Technical Parameters Recorded
1. **Exact Share and Volume:**
   - **Exact Share:** "70% WERE IN PROTRACTED SITUATIONS" (p. 3 Infographic); "Almost seven in ten refugees globally are in protracted situations" (p. 10).
   - **Absolute Number:** **24.9 million refugees** in protracted situations (p. 10).
2. **Definition of "Protracted":**
   - "Protracted situations are defined as those where refugees have been in exile for five years or more after their initial displacement, without immediate prospects for implementation of durable solutions" (p. 3, p. 10; citing ExCom Conclusion No. 109).
3. **Change to Definition / Minimum-Size Threshold:**
   - **Major methodological update in 2025:** UNHCR **eliminated the legacy 25,000-refugee minimum threshold**.
   - As explained on page 10:
     > "Previously, a threshold of 25,000 refugees was also considered to classify the situation as protracted. However, this excluded situations with refugees falling below it... In light of the adjusted definition, there were 24.9 million refugees in just over 1,300 protracted situations worldwide in 2025."
   - Under the old definition with the 25,000 cut-off (as reported in the 2024 report, p. 40), there were 57 protracted situations in 36 host countries totaling 24.7 million refugees (67%). Under the adjusted definition, the count captures over 1,300 situations globally totaling 24.9 million (70%).
4. **Reference Date:**
   - **End-2025** (data year 2025; report published June 2026).
5. **Population Scope (Refugees vs Other Groups):**
   - Footnote 34 (p. 10) explicitly defines the denominator:
     > "Includes people in a refugee-like situation and other people in need of international protection. Palestine refugees under UNRWA’s mandate are excluded from this analysis."
   - Does **not** include internally displaced persons (IDPs) or asylum-seekers whose status is pending.
- **Verdict:** **SUPPORTED [FULL-TEXT-READ]**.
- **Recommended Exact Slide Wording:**
  > "Seven in ten refugees (70%, 24.9M) are in protracted situations lasting five or more years (UNHCR Global Trends, end-2025 data; revised threshold-free definition)."

## 4. Claim 3: OCHA Humanitarian Needs in GHO 2026

### Slide Claim
> "about 239 million people need humanitarian assistance (Global Humanitarian Overview 2026)."

### Primary Source Verification
- **Primary Source:** United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA), *Global Humanitarian Overview 2026* `[FULL-TEXT-READ]`.
  - **Issuing Body:** United Nations OCHA.
  - **Date:** 8 December 2025.
  - **Primary URLs:**
    - Main Portal: [Humanitarian Action - GHO 2026](https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026/)
    - At a Glance: [GHO 2026 At a Glance](https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026/article/glance-17)
    - Full Article: [GHO 2026: A Collective Push to Protect Millions of Lives](https://humanitarianaction.info/article/2026-global-humanitarian-overview-collective-push-protect-millions-lives)
    - Trends Document: [GHO 2026 Trends in Crises and Needs](https://humanitarianaction.info/document/global-humanitarian-overview-2026/article/trends-crises-and-needs-world-breaking-point)
- **Location in Source:** "At a glance" key figures box; "Response in 2026" article, paragraph 2.
- **Short Quotation (<25 words):**
  > "In 2026, humanitarians will aim to collectively assist 135 million people, out of 239 million people in need" (para. 2).

### Key Technical Parameters Recorded
1. **Exact Number of People in Need:**
   - **239 million people** ("People in need at launch (Dec. 2025): 239 million").
2. **Number Targeted & Prioritized:**
   - **Targeted:** **135 million people** ("aim to collectively assist 135 million people").
   - **Hyper-prioritized (Immediate Life-Saving):** **87 million people** ("immediate priority being to save 87 million lives", reflecting intense prioritization under funding constraints).
3. **Funding Requirements:**
   - **Total Appeal:** **$33 billion** at launch ("humanitarians are appealing for $33 billion through the GHO").
   - **Immediate Prioritized Requirement:** **$23 billion** ("of which $23 billion is required immediately to respond to the most life-threatening needs").
4. **Date of Publication:**
   - **8 December 2025** (the global launch of the 2026 appeal).
5. **Geographic Scope (Countries and Plans):**
   - Covers **50 countries** across **29 response plans and appeals**:
     - 20 Humanitarian Needs and Response Plans (HNRPs)
     - 3 Flash Appeals
     - 6 Regional Refugee and Migrant Response Plans (RRPs/RMRPs) covering an additional 27 countries.
   - Regional scope spans Asia-Pacific, Europe, Latin America & Caribbean, Middle East & North Africa, Southern & Eastern Africa, and West & Central Africa.
- **Verdict:** **SUPPORTED [FULL-TEXT-READ]**.
- **Recommended Exact Slide Wording:**
  > "239 million people need humanitarian assistance across 50 countries; appeals target 135 million requiring $33B, with $23B prioritized for 87 million (OCHA, Global Humanitarian Overview 2026, Dec 2025 launch)."

## 5. Optional Parameter: Duration and Persistence of Crises

Both OCHA and UNHCR primary documents provide explicit, plain-language statements regarding crisis duration and appeal persistence:

1. **Length and Persistence of Humanitarian Appeals (OCHA GHO 2026):**
   - Source: OCHA GHO 2026, *Trends in crises and needs: a world at breaking point* (published 8 Dec 2025) `[FULL-TEXT-READ]`.
   - Primary statement:
     > "international humanitarian action in 75 per cent (18 out of 24) HNRP countries has lasted more than 10 years."
   - On conflict duration:
     > "Conflict duration has nearly doubled in the past thirty years: the average duration of conflicts that ended in 1990 was around 16 years, while in 2020 it was over 30 years."
2. **Duration of Individual Displacement in Asylum (UNHCR Global Trends 2025):**
   - Source: UNHCR *Global Trends 2025*, Chapter 1, Section "How long do individuals remain as refugees or asylum-seekers?", p. 11 `[FULL-TEXT-READ]`.
   - Primary statement:
     > "Overall, the median time spent in asylum by people registered as refugees or asylum-seekers in Eastern and Southern Africa between 2001 and 2025 was just under 16 years (see figure 2)."
