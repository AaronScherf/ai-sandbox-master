## 2. A definition point worth settling before the Egger versus HSNP comparison

- **Egger** defines its multiplier M as the cumulative effect on **local real GDP** per unit transferred, and estimates it two ways (expenditure and income), both near 2.5 **[C]**.
- **HSNP**'s evaluation reports that "overall income rises by between 0.38 and 0.93 KES" per KES. The methodology text you pasted says an income multiplier is the impact on total income divided by the transfer, and gives the example that a multiplier of 1.5 means each KES generates an **additional** 0.5 KES of income **[C, from your paste; I have not located the LEWIE methods page in the transcripts]**.
- **Reading [I]:** if the Completion Review's 0.38 to 0.93 is the *additional* income beyond the transfer, the comparable total multiplier would be about 1.38 to 1.93, not 0.38 to 0.93, which would narrow the apparent gap with Egger's 2.5. If it is already a total multiplier, the gap stands. The text I have does not settle which, so **the first harmonization step is to read the LEWIE methods and confirm the convention.** Even then the two differ in design (a randomized trial with measured spillovers versus a simulation model of the local economy) and in the quantity (GDP versus household income).
- **Effect on the slide [P]:** the slide's wording, "the measures are defined differently; why they differ is not established", remains right. Avoid showing 0.38 to 0.93 beside 2.5 without a conversion note. The speaker note now says so.
