## 1. Diagnosis (condensed)

**[V]** v3 correctly centers the prolonged-crisis choice, treats the decision as hypothetical, demotes the simulator, and adds a qualified JMMI example. Its slides 10–11 are sound.

**[I]** Slides 12 and 13 lost focus because (a) slide 12 turned structural breaks from a property of the causal model (a *mechanism* differs) into a monitoring point (the panel watches repeated shocks), dropping treatment version, DAG and interference; and (b) slide 13 replaced the method-selection slot with a new substantive domain, so slide 14's brief had no method to be the output of.

**Through-line:** for a specified decision, ask what differs between source and target, which differences the data can diagnose, what local learning could close the gap, and whether the answer changes a choice once local constraints are known.

## 2. Agenda: a staged pipeline in the order a real partnership would run

The owner's extension [O] changes the shape of the agenda. Framing C was two questions (transfer, then use). It becomes a **sequence of stages, each with its own question and standalone output**, ordered by the steps taken with a real partner. Labels RQ1–RQ3 are kept as the owner assigned them, so the *run order is RQ3 → RQ2 → RQ1*. (Open choice: relabel by run order to avoid confusion; see §8.)

| Run order | Label | Question | Method character | Standalone output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1** | **RQ3** | What does the decision-maker already know or have access to, and would new evidence (or a more accessible version of existing evidence) be marginally worth the time and effort to collect, analyze and understand? | Qualitative; knowledge-management / information audit | A go/no-go on further evidence work, or a "make existing information usable" recommendation |
| **Stage 2** | **RQ2** | What are the constraints and discretion of this specific decision, and which interventions would be considered under which conditions? | Participatory DAG mapping and scenario planning | Scenario-specific log-frames; per-node data inventory and decision thresholds; constraint-first screen |
| **Stage 3** | **RQ1** | Given the decision space, which sources are informative, how far are they from the target (spatial, temporal, institutional, market, treatment version, population, interference), and which output tier does the evidence support? | Transportability diagnostics, then computation | Output tier (estimate / bounds / mechanism hypothesis / no inference) and, where supported, the computed output |
| **Stage 3b** | – | Compute the supported output | Method depends on data | Target estimate, bounds, or scenario-conditional ranges |
| **Gate** | – | *Current data insufficient*: which specific gaps block inference, and how could each be remedied? | Gap register | Ranked remedies (§2.4) |
| **After** | – | What happened once the decision was applied, and was the decision process useful? | Prospective evaluation design (§2.6) | Evidence on the intervention and on the process |

### 2.1 Stage 1 (RQ3): is new evidence worth it?

**[P]** This is the gate the 2×2 was missing. The original deck and v3 both *presuppose* that evidence work is needed; the 2×2 (find, describe, map, replicate) is only worth running if this stage says yes. Two parts:
- *Inventory:* what does the decision-maker already have (programme monitoring, assessments, prior evaluations, institutional memory, donor requirements), what can they actually access, and in what form?
- *Marginal value:* would more evidence change anything, given the cost to collect, analyze and understand it? That is a value-of-information question. **[I]** A formal expected-value-of-information calculation needs a payoff model the project does not have, so the first version should be qualitative: *Does the surviving choice set contain options the existing information cannot separate? Is the deadline compatible with collecting more?*

Possible results include "no new evidence needed," "existing evidence needs to be made more accessible" (a legitimate deliverable by itself), and "targeted new evidence is worth it, here."

### 2.2 Stage 2 (RQ2): decision space

**[O → P]** Two linked tools:
1. **Participatory DAG mapping:** with decision-makers, map the relevant information and hypothesized causal linkages for the choice.
2. **Scenario planning:** identify what intervention is feasible under which conditions (conflict intensifies and supply chains break; the border closes; funding lapses). **Each scenario gets its own log-frame**, and for each node the team records (a) what data exist to inform it and (b) the **thresholds that create cutoffs and force decisions**. That models constraints directly rather than as a prose "feasibility" note.

**[I]** This stage feeds Stage 3 twice. It defines the **target claim** (which option, in which scenario, with which outcome), and it fixes which dimensions of distance matter most. A scenario in which the market link fails makes market distance decisive; one with a closed border makes spatial and institutional distance decisive.

*Note on human subjects [O]:* participatory work with practitioners is human-subjects research and waits. The desk-based version derives DAGs, scenarios and log-frames from programme documents.

### 2.3 Stage 3 (RQ1): transfer diagnostics and computation

Unchanged in substance from the earlier review, with the dimensions restructured (§3). The distinction between *categorizing* the supported output and *computing* it is made explicit: categorization is a diagnostic of the sources and the target; computation is a separate, data-dependent method choice (slide 13 material).

**The "no inference" case is not empty [O].** It still yields a rich evidence synthesis, a context data space, log-frame models and scenario plans with hypothesized causal chains, which have independent value to a decision-maker. This also answers the worry that a null tier makes the project look unproductive.

### 2.4 The "current data insufficient" gate

**[O → P]** When evidence cannot support inference, the output is a **gap register**: each missing item, which node or link it affects, and a remedy. The owner's examples, with my annotations:

| Remedy | Fits gaps in | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot or small trial | Treatment-version, delivery-institution or mechanism links | Needs partner and, with participants, ethics review |
| Targeted survey | Outcomes; population; market access | Cost and security |
| Custom geodata (e.g. drone flyover) | Spatial and environmental detail; physical access | Permissions, cost, airspace **[I]** |
| Data request to another organization | Administrative, payment, price or assessment data | The JMMI review shows reuse rights can be unresolved **[V]** |
| Adjusted monitoring, including rapid AI-assisted feedback | Fast, repeated signals during the programme | Elrha's AINAA is a *project page* with intended outputs and no published delivery updates, so it is a comparator, not a validated tool **[V: v3 §5]**. It is a separate item from the *From knowing to doing* review, which concerns evidence use. |

### 2.5 Portfolio and iteration

**[O → P]** Two additions:
- **Related decisions.** Run the same stages across a set of linked decisions that share data and test for **synergies or conflicts** (if A for decision alpha, decision beta may no longer be relevant, and gamma should take C). **[I]** This is a decision-dependency graph on top of the per-decision log-frames. It is also a scope risk: start with one decision and make the portfolio layer a documented extension.
- **Iteration.** After new evidence is delivered, return to Stage 1: is yet more information worth it? Decisions that reached the data gate are the main reason to loop.

### 2.6 Outcomes and evaluation: closing the loop

**[O → P]** If the decision-maker is still in the planning phase and a causal model and context data already exist, the intervention can be **designed to be evaluable from the start** (a small RCT or pilot, a stepped rollout, or difference-in-differences), for the intervention if it is amenable to one. This serves two purposes:

1. **Did the intervention work?**
2. **Did the transfer claim hold?** **[I]** This may be the strongest benefit: pre-stating the Stage-3 prediction (tier, bounds, sign) and comparing it with the realized effect is a *prospective* validation of the transport claim, which reduces reliance on leave-one-trial-out checks (a stress test, not proof) **[V: follow-up critique]**.

*Whether the decision process helped* is the harder meta-question. **[I]** With one decision there is no counterfactual process. Options that could approximate one: pre-registered predictions scored for calibration; contrasting related decisions or sites that used the pipeline with those that did not (a stepped introduction); and comparing the process record (options before and after) against the outcome. Each is weak alone. Caveats: randomization within a live crisis programme can be infeasible or ethically constrained, and a well-evaluated programme may be unrepresentative of typical ones.

## 3. Dimensions of transfer distance and the panels each needs

**[O]** The causal transfer question has temporal, spatial, institutional and market dynamics. I add that environmental dynamics sit inside the spatial dimension but have their own temporal component: seasonality, ENSO-type supercycles, and climate-trend feedbacks. The earlier review's six links (treatment version, mechanism, delivery institution, market/payment links, displacement, interference) remain as the *nodes*; the dimensions below describe *how they differ across source and target*.

| Dimension | What differs | Data character | Existing asset **[V]** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spatial (incl. environmental)** | Agro-ecology, terrain, access, hazard exposure, settlement and displacement pattern | Gridded or admin-level panels (climate, land, conflict, displacement) | Compound-shocks panel (Somalia/Sudan): context only, no interventions or outcomes |
| **Temporal** | Source period vs. target period; seasonality; multi-year cycles; **non-stationarity** (a source from a different climate or conflict regime) | Long time-series, with repeated cross-sections of shocks | Panel covers a limited window; adequacy for supercycles unestablished **[I]** |
| **Institutional** | NGO vs. public delivery, registries, payment rails, mandate, funding windows | Mostly coded qualitative or documentary data; rarely a panel | None. A coded institutional panel would be new **[I]** |
| **Market** | Prices, functionality, pass-through, supply response, payment reach | Price and market-functionality panels | WFP prices in panel; JMMI is a qualified candidate and **not** in production **[V]** |
| **Treatment / population / interference** | Version, who is present, spillovers | Programme records, trial microdata, endline tracking | Not held. Bangladesh replication is gated on village coordinates **[V]** |

**Implication for slide 12:** it should be organized by these dynamics: *the structural break is a node where the source-target difference is in the mechanism, not just the covariates*, with environmental and temporal non-stationarity added to the market and institutional examples already drafted. That strengthens the slide.

## 7. Smallest credible next step (revised)

**A desk-based retrospective audit of one richly documented programme.** **[O]** guidance: *not* a small trial such as the Bangladesh one, because ex ante alternatives are unknown. The search is held for the next step. Criteria drawn from the owner's description:

1. **Design documentation that traces evidence use** for a specific intervention set (what evidence was consulted, what alternatives were considered).
2. **Monitoring and evaluation data across the programme's lifetime**, ideally a closeout evaluation that can be traced back to design decisions.
3. **Geographic specificity**, so spatial, environmental, market and institutional distance can be assessed.
4. **Sufficient scale** that the choice set was real.
5. Preferably within the cash vs. safety net family.

**[I]** Two procedural cautions: (a) *hindsight bias:* use only information available at the design date, and date-stamp each source; (b) *selection:* programmes with excellent documentation and M&E are unrepresentative, so conclusions should be stated for that kind of programme. I have not verified that any specific programme meets all five criteria, and I name none here.

If found, the audit would run the stages retrospectively: what the decision-maker knew (Stage 1), the decision space and scenarios reconstructed from documents (Stage 2), the transfer audit of the sources available at the time (Stage 3), and whether closeout results trace to design choices. A later, optional step is contacting the partner to validate details. No human-subjects work is involved at this stage **[O]**. Nothing here changes the JMMI production gate, the Bangladesh linkage gate or any approval.
