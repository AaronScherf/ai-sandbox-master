## 1. The partner funnel as an RQ1 observable [O]

**Owner's design:** approach multiple partners. Most will not respond, some will refuse outright, a handful will accept. Analyzing that funnel is a useful check on RQ1. Continue until at least one partner agrees (preferably more) **and** is judged a good fit for the remaining questions, meaning it has a set of decisions and potential program designs or adaptations.

**Effect on the validation definition [I]:** this resolves the v4 tension. RQ1 condition (b) can be read as *"at least one fit partner agrees, and the funnel is documented."* Non-acceptances are data, not failures of validation.

**Recorded stages [P]:** approached → responded → declined / no response → accepted → judged a fit (has a decision set). Where a reason is given for declining, record it (no need for evidence, no time, no trust, wrong mandate, legal limits, not a research partner).

**Cautions [I]:**
- The funnel measures demand for *this offer from this researcher*. "No response" is weakly informative: it cannot separate "we have enough evidence" from "no time" or "do not know you." Treat it as one signal alongside the desk audit's support-seeking check (v4 §4).
- "Until at least one partner agrees" has no bound. A time-box and a review point protect the PhD calendar. The timeline in §3 suggests one.
- Whether outreach to organizations falls under human-subjects review is an IRB question, not mine. Ask early, since it affects when outreach can start relative to the approval cycle.
- Add a "fit" checklist before approaching: has a decision set, a planning-phase window, authority to adapt, and data or documents it is willing to share.

## 4. Saturation as its own subproject [O]

**Owner's observation:** defining saturation needs research first. An initial review of 20 papers might identify 30 interventions, each appearing in some fraction of papers, plus a list of outcomes with the fraction of papers mentioning each. Outcome *values* are noisier but more useful for later phases. Defining the standards precisely is a subproject; step one is to check whether the meta-analysis field has already done it.

**Proposed shape [P]:** two layers of stability across search sweeps.
1. **Coverage stability:** the set of interventions, outcomes and settings, and their frequencies across papers (descriptive, cheap).
2. **Value stability:** the distribution of effect estimates per intervention–outcome pair (noisier, relevant to RQ3 priors).

A similarity measure between successive sweeps would compare these distributions, and saturation would be declared on a pre-stated tolerance. The tolerance cannot be set until the literature check is done.

**Pointers to check [I, from my own knowledge, not verified]:** stopping rules and search-sufficiency methods in systematic reviews; capture–recapture and species-accumulation approaches to estimating how many relevant studies remain unfound; cumulative and trial-sequential meta-analysis for stability of pooled estimates; and information saturation in qualitative synthesis. I do not know which, if any, fits an intervention-by-outcome taxonomy like the 3ie one the evidence map uses.
