## 2. A plain-language framing: supply, demand and room to choose

**Core idea (from value of information):** evidence is only worth paying for if it could change what the planner does. Economists will recognise this as the expected value of information; the audience of economists and environmental scientists also knows it from adaptive management and robust decision-making under uncertainty. Sources already in the project set: Claxton (1999), Fenwick et al. (2008), Brown et al. (2025), IDinsight (2022). Other names I could add are from memory and unverified.

**Three conditions for evidence to be in demand [I]:**
1. **Room to choose** (technical term: discretion). The planner's choice set is not pinned by rules, budget, mandate or norms. This is not "agency" or "flexibility"; it is the space that remains after constraints.
2. **Uncertainty about which option is best.** A planner with room to choose and a confident prior has no demand.
3. **Evidence that could shift that belief**, which depends on relevance (below).

**Relevance has two parts [I]:**
- **Distance:** will the effect found elsewhere hold here? (transportability; RQ3)
- **Actionability:** can this planner implement it at all? (feasibility under local constraints; RQ2's constraint-first screen)

**Decision table (a planner's decision falls in one cell):**

| Relevant evidence exists? | Room to choose: **yes** | Room to choose: **no** (fixed by rules, budget, mandate) |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes: relevant and actionable** | **Use it** | On the shelf |
| **Yes, but too distant** (different context) | **Gap: transport the evidence (RQ3)** | Not a priority |
| **Yes, but not actionable** here | **Gap: redesign or adapt (RQ2)** | Not a priority |
| **None** | **Gap: generate or substitute (data-insufficient register)** | Ignore |

The research question is the **share of a planner's decisions in each cell**, from the planner's perspective (the desk audit tally and the partner funnel measure it). Relevance is a continuum; the table simplifies it, and the transport distance is the formal version of that continuum.

**Scope choice [O/P]:** target decisions where the planner **already has room to choose** and lacks relevant evidence, not the argument that evidence should loosen constraints. The latter (evidence relaxing constraints) is a plausible link but is not tested; state it as out of scope. This matches the working hypothesis that nexus planners have some room to choose and that existing evidence is either too distant or not actionable.

**Other states of the world (not exhaustive):** little demand beyond logistics; abundant evidence not used; evidence used broadly and effectively; planners make do and would do better with more relevant evidence; insufficient evidence either way. The table above says which of these is happening, per decision.

## 3. Why transportability, stated specifically
- **General argument [C]:** Pritchett and Sandefur (2015) compare errors from using experiments in other contexts with the bias of local observational estimates (microcredit). Across the cases they study, the transport error is in most cases larger than the local bias. They also cite Bold et al. on Kenyan education, where NGO and government implementation of the same programme gave different results, an implementer-capacity example **[C for the citation; I have not read Bold et al.]**.
- **Within a single paper [C]:** Crost, Felter and Johnston (2016) find cash transfers reduced conflict incidents, and contrast this with community-driven development, which other work found increased conflict. Intervention type flipped the sign in the same country.
- **Missing [I]:** a documented case in a fragile or conflict setting where evidence from a different setting was rejected or failed for a stated reason (state capacity, markets, security). The Nigeria-to-Sudan intuition is anecdotal. A short search is in progress (`context-distance-cases` handoff). Until then, present the intuition as the motivation, not as a result.
