## 1. Evidence-use picture after the adversarial scan [S]

The fresh session found sources in most other states, so the first scan's near-uniform result (14 of 16 in one category) does not stand as a description of the field.

| State | Sources | What they say |
|---|---|---|
| Abundant evidence, not used (B) | 4 (Barder et al. 2015; CALP 2020; Itad 2018; Green and Knox Clarke 2021) | For cash assistance, about 15 years of strong evidence did not drive uptake; institutional inertia, silos and procurement lock-in did. |
| Little demand beyond logistics (A) | 2 (Campbell and Knox Clarke 2019; Knox Clarke and Darcy 2014) | Only about 19% of recorded field decisions concern intervention options or targeting; most are administrative or logistical. |
| Used effectively (C) | 2 (ComPAS trial, South Sudan/Kenya; Mercy Corps adolescent support in Jordan) | Impact evaluations changed programme design and later guidelines. |
| Constraints bind, not evidence (E) | 2 (ALNAP SOHS 2022; graduation pilots for refugees) | Access denial, counter-terrorism law, funding gaps and legal limits dominate. |
| Evidence travels | 2 (Doocy and Tappis 2016; Kurdi 2021, Yemen) | Design features mattered more than emergency context; a cash-plus design worked in a civil-war setting. |
| Evidence deficit (D, the working hypothesis) | 2 (Gentilini 2016; Dempster and Herbert 2023) | Comparative evidence outside food assistance is sparse. |
| Insufficient to judge (F) | 0 | |

**Reading [I]:** the pattern differs by **sector and decision type**: for cash and food, evidence is plentiful and under-used; for health, shelter, education and refugee self-reliance, it is sparse. So "planners lack relevant evidence" is not a field-wide fact; it is a share-by-cell question, which is what the decision table in v7 §2 measures.

**Cautions:** the same ALNAP study is counted as "little demand" here and as supporting the working hypothesis in the first scan, and its sample is given as 50 managers here and 55 decision-makers earlier; classification depends on the reader. The 19% figure is new and unchecked **[S]**; if it holds, it shows the relevant denominator is a minority of decisions, which matters for scope. I have not read any of the 15 sources.

**Stated non-findings [S]:** no documented case of planners shelving designs because an impact evaluation was unavailable; no evidence of formal value-of-information calculations in conflict settings; no cross-site heterogeneity studies for non-food sectors in active conflict.

## 2. Planner discretion is now the main empirical risk
The scan reports **no evidence of an unconstrained planner decision space**: choices are bounded upstream by donor log-frames and short earmarks [S]. This challenges the premise I recommended for Friday ("target decisions where the planner already has discretion"). It does not defeat it, because the RQ1 screen already asks whether discretion remains after constraints (v5 §2), but the talk should treat discretion as **something the audit measures**, not an assumption.

**Wording [P]:** "Planners are bound by rules, budgets and norms. The question is how much discretion remains on the design margin, and on that margin, whether the available evidence is relevant enough to use." If discretion is small in practice, the answer to RQ1 is "mostly not worth seeking new evidence", which is a valid result and the stop outcome of the RQ1 screen.

The decision table is unchanged (v7 §2) with "room to choose" renamed **planner discretion** (columns: discretion yes / constrained).

## 3. Distance cases for the transportability question [S unless noted]

| Case | What it shows | Measured or asserted | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bold et al. (2018, JPubE)**, Kenya teacher contracts | Large effect under NGO delivery, zero when run by the ministry at scale; state capacity and politics | Measured, randomised (192 schools) | **Best distance case** |
| **Crost, Felter and Johnston (2014, AER)** vs **(2016, JDE)**, Philippines | Community-driven development increased conflict through insurgent response [C for the contrast in the 2016 paper's text]; cash transfers decreased it [C] | Measured | Intervention type and armed actors flip the sign |
| Humphreys et al. (2019), DRC Tuungane | Community-driven programme built infrastructure but produced no gain in cohesion or governance amid militia dynamics | Measured, randomised | Context mattered |
| Berman et al. (2011), Afghanistan, Iraq, Philippines | Peacetime opportunity-cost logic failed in conflict | Measured | Theory does not transport |
| Blattman et al. (2017), Liberia | Cash alone did not reduce crime; pairing with cognitive behavioural therapy did | Measured | Package needed |
| South Sudan appraisal and a BASIC Research brief | Planners rejected government delivery; proxy-means testing models fail in fluid crises | Asserted / synthesised | Planner view |
| **Counter-case: Bedoya et al. (2019), Afghanistan** | A multi-component graduation programme that worked in six peaceful countries also worked under active insurgency (reported 30% consumption gain) | Measured, randomised | **Evidence can travel** |

**Corrected: Egger versus Kenya HSNP.** Gemini's note contrasts Egger et al.'s multiplier of about 2.5 with 0.38 to 0.93 in the HSNP Phase 2 local-economy evaluation and attributes the gap to market isolation. I checked the Completion Review **[C]**: it reports "overall income rises by between 0.38 and 0.93 KES" per KES injected, and frames this positively. It does **not** attribute the gap to infrastructure or market isolation, and the two figures are different quantities (income per shilling in four counties; import-adjusted expenditure multiplier in Egger). **Do not present this as a transport failure.** If used at all: "different settings and methods gave different multipliers; the explanation is not established."

**Reading [I]:** the cases show both failures and a success, which is what the transportability question needs. The research question is not "nothing travels" but **"can we say beforehand how far a given finding travels, and for which decisions."** The Bedoya case is a strong reason not to over-claim. Numbers in this table are unchecked by me except where marked.

## 4. Are the research questions worth asking?

| RQ | Assessment on current evidence [I] |
|---|---|
| **RQ3 transfer** | **Yes.** Documented failures, a documented success, and a general theory of why distance matters (Pritchett and Sandefur [C]) together motivate a method for predicting how far findings travel. Weakest point: the Nigeria-to-Sudan state-capacity intuition is supported by Bold (Kenya) and DRC but not by a fragile-state-to-fragile-state case. |
| **RQ2 decision space** | **Yes, conditionally.** Constraints are heavy and upstream; mapping them is itself informative. Partial overlap with scenario-planning literature. |
| **RQ1 knowledge audit** | **Worth asking, not yet shown to be needed.** Distinct from the nearest prior art, and the stop outcome handles the "discretion is small" and "evidence is not the binding constraint" cases. The scan finds both. The honest framing: RQ1 tests which of these situations holds for a given decision. |

The strongest honest statement for Friday: the questions are worth asking **because the field shows mixed, sector-dependent patterns that nobody has measured decision by decision**, not because a gap has been proven.
