### 2×2 buildout and saturation (the added milestone)

The four processes correspond to the 2×2 quadrants: literature review and synthesis (1.1), data fusion (1.2), evidence gap mapping (2.1), and replication (2.2) **[V: strategy v3 §3]**. Current maturity **[V: tracker]**: the compound-shocks panel is built and reproducing; the evidence map is in brainstorming; the Bangladesh replication has a village-coordinate gate; the data-fusion toolkit is deferred; the transferability line is planned with no repository.

**Saturation criterion [P].** The earlier "asymptotically complete corpus" claim was removed because it had no stopping rule. Replace it with a **pre-stated rule relative to a specific target claim**, not to the whole field: after each iteration sweep, record (i) new relevant sources found per sweep, (ii) changes to the gap map, and (iii) whether the output tier for any node changed. Declare saturation when several consecutive sweeps change none of these beyond a stated tolerance. This is a rule *for a case*, so saturation depends on the decision from RQ2; the buildout cannot be fully separated from the audit. It also yields something reportable: the rate at which new evidence stops arriving.

**Dependency [I].** Replication and data fusion are the slow, partially gated components (coordinates, access, licensing), so saturation may be limited by data access rather than search. The gap register (v3 §2) should record a *blocked* status separately from a *saturated* one.

## 4. Desk audit: support-seeking check and the selection problem

**Owner's addition [O]:** during the desk audit, record whether decision-makers sought outside support for evidence gathering or analysis (hired research consultants, drew on home-office support), and under what conditions. If budget data are available, price it, or scrape a database of procurement notices for contracted evidence support.

**Why it helps [I]:** it observes the revealed demand for the kind of help the agenda proposes without needing a volunteer partner, so it can include decision-makers who did *not* seek help and therefore partially answers the selection concern. It is also a revealed-preference version of the willingness-to-pay idea the owner parked.

**Checks to include [P]:**
- Which decisions had external evidence support and which did not; what features distinguish them (size, timeline, novelty, donor requirement).
- Whether the support was commissioned before or after the design decision (timing matters for hindsight bias).
- If budget lines exist, the cost and scope of the support.
- Procurement notices: the source audit found UN procurement awards are public on UNGM but incomplete (most, not all UN organizations; thresholds apply; some supplier names withheld) **[V: source verification]**, so any scrape needs a coverage audit. I have not checked other procurement portals.

**Ranking of selection-bias approaches [O → I]:**
1. **Desk audit with support-seeking check.** Low cost, observable with existing information. Do first.
2. **Experimentally designed intake form.** Could be interesting; keep as an option after the desk work.
3. **A service provider's recruitment pipeline** (how many leads become clients vs. the target pool). A digression; the owner judges it likely more trouble than it is worth if the desk work answers RQ1. Save as follow-on, together with survey or experimental work. Someone has probably studied the general case; a quick literature check is cheap **[I]**.

## 5. Portfolio and iteration, built in [O]

**Portfolio.** A partner designing a program may have 10–15 overlapping interventions and will want support for as many as possible. So the unit of analysis is a **set of linked decisions**, with dependency (if A for alpha, then beta is moot; do C for gamma). **Descriptive output [O]:** the fraction of decisions in the set reaching each type of transportability outcome (estimate / bounds / mechanism hypothesis / no inference). It is a small, clearly biased sample; report it as descriptive.

**Iteration.** Support is staged, and each stage is a discrete deliverable updated with the partner to validate its relevance **before** the next one:
1. Evidence gap analysis
2. Literature synthesis
3. Log-frame modeling and scenario planning
4. Relevance check with the decision-maker
5. RQ3 causal transport model and predictions
6. Follow-up questions on the predictions

The whole pipeline is **not** repeated with the same decision-maker in this plan [O]. Iteration is within stages (and for new evidence feeding back to RQ1 as a screen), not a full second pass.

**Risk [I]:** 10–15 interventions times several stages is a large workload for one researcher. Use the RQ1 screen to **triage** which decisions get RQ3, and state this in the proposal.

## 6. Predictions from literature priors

**Owner's idea [O]:** priors from the literature review supply the basis for each prediction. Illustration given: a source LATE of 10 percentage points of mitigation (standard error 2) becomes a predicted LATE of about 5 with standard error about 5, which is testable.

**Cautions [I]:**
- The shrinkage factor and the inflated uncertainty should themselves be set *by rule from the literature* (for example, observed attenuation and between-study heterogeneity across similar settings) and pre-registered. If they are chosen after seeing anything, they can be tuned to fit.
- With a standard error of 5 around a point estimate of 5, the 95% interval roughly spans zero (about −5 to 15). A hit in that interval is a weak test. The pre-specified informativeness criterion (v3 §3.4) should say what the prediction rules out, such as a naive baseline or zero.
- The ex post evaluation also needs enough power. A power calculation for the planned evaluation should come before the prediction is called testable.
- The numbers above are the owner's illustration, not estimates from any source.

### Transfer card, explained (to answer your question)

The "transfer card" is my name (from v1, slide 14) for a **one-page summary per intervention option**, listing: what was delivered in the source evidence; link-by-link comparability to the target (measured / testable locally / unidentified); local tests and monitoring indicators; feasibility vetoes; the evidence-support status of that option (supported / contested / unidentified, with no ranking); and "what would change this conclusion?". "Placement" meant *which slide shows it* in the talk. After your description of staged deliverables, I think the better framing is that the card is simply the **RQ3 output format** within stage 5 of §5, so it needs no slide of its own: show it once on the pipeline slide (5) or the method slide (10). That drops the three-option choice from v3.
