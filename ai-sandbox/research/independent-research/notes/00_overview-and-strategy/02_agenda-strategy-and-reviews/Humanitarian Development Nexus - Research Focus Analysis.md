# Research focus analysis: external evidence in prolonged crisis and the humanitarian–development nexus

*Exploratory analysis, 2026-11-09. This note responds to the user's proposed shift in emphasis. It identifies research questions and interfaces; it does not revise the presentation deck, select a dissertation design, or approve a project sequence.*

## The shift in the motivating question

The [original presentation narrative](Research%20Presentation%20Narrative%20-%20Humanitarian%20Transportability%20Pipeline.md) opens with a planner in a sudden emergency who needs to choose among interventions before reading the literature. The [revised strategy](Humanitarian%20Evidence%20Agenda%20-%20Updated%20Presentation%20Strategy.md) separates immediate response, protracted adaptation, and advance planning, but still gives the flash-response dilemma the opening and much of the visual weight. The [critique analysis](Humanitarian%20Evidence%20Agenda%20-%20Critique%20Analysis.md) and [source audit](Humanitarian%20Evidence%20Agenda%20-%20Source%20Verification.md) make the acute-window claim hard to carry as the agenda's core: a local access or delivery constraint can dominate an externally estimated treatment effect, and the strongest claims about universal planner behavior were not established.

The user's proposed center is more precise: **what can researchers contribute when a crisis continues long enough for institutions to choose, revise, and combine interventions, yet the setting is too unstable or data-poor for ordinary development-program assumptions?** This is the space between the first relief shipment and a stable long-run development program. It includes repeated drought and conflict exposure, displacement and return, market repair, social protection, livelihood recovery, anticipatory financing, and protecting earlier development gains from the next shock.

This is a stronger reason to study transportability than “planners need a faster RCT lookup.” The user of evidence may be a government ministry designing an adaptive safety net, a donor setting a multi-year funding window, a humanitarian–development coordination group, a local implementer negotiating changes, or an insurer pricing a pre-arranged payout. The decision could be whether to extend cash, add productive support, shift targeting, invest in a payment rail, pre-position a contingency fund, or change a trigger. Those choices have different evidence requirements, and some remain constrained by politics, safety, and institutional mandates. The research should investigate **where real discretion exists** rather than assume an open portfolio.

## “Nexus” needs a research definition

The nexus is not a single chronological handoff from relief to development. OECD's [DAC Recommendation](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-5019) describes context-specific collaboration, shared analysis, and collective outcomes across different mandates; its [interim review](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-humanitarian-development-peace-nexus-interim-progress-review_2f620ca5-en.html) finds gaps between those ambitions and coordination, programming, and financing practice. A place can have emergency distributions, public services, displacement, livelihood investments, and peace risks at the same time. A new shock can move the relevant decision back toward immediate relief without making earlier recovery questions disappear. OECD and UNHCR's [forced-displacement review](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-humanitarian-development-peace-nexus-and-forced-displacement_3e493170-en.html) examines programming, coordination, and financing across 27 initiatives; it is a useful institutional comparator, not proof of a universal pathway.

For this agenda, an **operational definition** could be the set of decisions where (a) acute needs persist or recur, (b) the choice changes medium-term welfare, capacity, or exposure to the next shock, and (c) more than one actor or funding logic is involved. That definition is proposed here as a way to select cases, not as an established sector standard. It directs attention to three interfaces:

| Interface | Example decision | Evidence tension |
|---|---|---|
| Relief to sustained protection | Continue emergency cash, hand beneficiaries to a public safety net, or add livelihood support? | Immediate coverage and protection versus longer-run welfare, targeting, and fiscal durability. |
| Repeated shocks to preparedness | Fund pre-positioned cash systems, insurance, or trigger-based support before the next drought? | Forecast calibration and basis risk versus the value of earlier action and avoided asset loss. |
| Displacement to recovery | Support return, local integration, or services in host areas? | Movement and security change the target population, outcome horizon, and ethical meaning of “success.” |

The World Bank's [adaptive social protection framework](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/human-capital/publication/adaptive-social-protection-building-resilience-to-shocks-key-findings) is a concrete example of this interface: programs, information, financing, and institutions connect regular support to shock response. Its [Sahel program](https://www.worldbank.org/en/programs/sahel-adaptive-social-protection-program-trust-fund/overview) combines safety nets, economic inclusion, and temporary shock support. These are examples of existing approaches and possible research partners or comparators; they do not establish that the proposed work is novel or that any one design is optimal. The question for this agenda is where externally learned effects and local implementation evidence add value to those choices.

## Where an academic researcher may help

The interface between academic and practitioner is not a model that delivers a single “best intervention.” It is a **jointly specified claim**: a decision-maker names a choice and outcome; the researcher identifies the relevant evidence, the assumptions required to adapt it, and what local observations would support or refute those assumptions. Elrha's [*From knowing to doing*](https://www.elrha.org/resource/from-knowing-to-doing-evidence-use-in-the-humanitarian-sector) identifies time, funding, relevance, and weak relevance to Global South actors as barriers to research use, and names partnerships, evidence brokering, translation, humanitarian data, and leadership as responses. This supports treating the evidence-use relationship itself as a research object, while remembering that the report is a literature review and consultation rather than an experimental estimate of the value of brokering.

Four potential contributions fit the user's comparative advantage without requiring a career in corridor-level logistics:

1. **Choice-specific synthesis.** Search impact evaluations, gray evaluations, implementation studies, and local administrative or market data for a particular decision—for example, cash plus livelihood support versus cash alone in a recurrent drought setting. Organize evidence by treatment version, population, shock, outcome horizon, delivery institution, and reasons effects may differ. This is more demanding than counting studies in an intervention × outcome map but can build on the planned evidence-map line.
2. **Transportability diagnostics.** Before predicting a target effect, check whether the treatment and outcome are comparable, whether the target covariates overlap those of source studies, whether the mechanism depends on a functioning market or payment system, and whether displacement or spillovers change the unit of analysis. [Vivalt (2020)](https://academic.oup.com/jeea/article-abstract/18/6/3045/5908781) finds substantial heterogeneity in impact evaluation results and systematic differences associated with implementing organization; [Pritchett and Sandefur (2015)](https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.p20151016) formalize the policy problem when context matters. These results motivate diagnostics; they do not validate any particular crisis transport formula.
3. **Local updating and prospective learning.** During a prolonged crisis, monitoring, repeated surveys where safe, administrative records, and qualitative implementation evidence can test parts of a borrowed mechanism. A researcher can specify what would change the recommendation and when the evidence is insufficient. A hierarchy of possible outputs runs from descriptive comparison, to plausible bounds, to an identifiable target effect—only when the relevant assumptions and data support each step. The [prior RCT critique](../../03_rct-transportability/Follow-up%20-%20Data%20Availability%20and%20Transportability%20Critique.md) already stresses that replication, proxy response, welfare-surrogacy validation, and cross-site prediction are separate tests.
4. **Evidence-use evaluation.** Compare decisions made with and without a tailored evidence brief or structured case comparison, where feasible and ethical; trace whether evidence changed an option, a timing choice, or only a justification. The relevant outcome is not just whether the analyst produced an estimate but whether a locally accountable decision process improved. Co-design with practitioners and crisis-affected people is necessary to define that outcome. A negative result—no decision changed despite better evidence—would directly test the agenda's motivating claim.

This preserves a role for RCTs without making them a universal entry ticket. A trial can estimate a source effect or reveal a mechanism; target-site inference requires more. Conversely, local operational data can show market access or implementation fidelity without identifying welfare effects. The strongest contribution may be **when to borrow, adapt, combine, or decline to borrow external evidence**. It could involve methods, an empirical comparison of decisions, or a reproducible evidence product. Which of those becomes the central research contribution remains open.

## Research ideas within the new center

The [projects tracker](../../../PROJECTS.md) indexes existing work at different maturity levels. The compound-shocks panel is built and reproducing; `reprokit` v0.4.0 is released; the evidence map remains in brainstorming; the Bangladesh insurance replication has a village-coordinate gate; transferability has no dedicated repo; and the data-fusion toolkit is deferred. These statuses matter: a coherent narrative can connect the lines without claiming the integrated system exists.

**Repeated shocks and adaptation.** The panel's climate, conflict, displacement, and price measures can characterize changing exposure in Somalia and Sudan. A research question is whether external intervention evidence becomes more or less applicable as markets and displacement change across seasons. A district-month panel describes context; it does not establish local treatment assignment, delivery fidelity, or household welfare. A future study would need an intervention, timing, outcome, and comparison that are actually observed. The panel can still identify where a proposed transfer would require implausible overlap or where a shock invalidates a prior assumption.

**Adaptive social protection and economic inclusion.** This is a promising substantive nexus case because ongoing support, productive inclusion, and temporary shock transfers coexist. Questions include when an emergency transfer should become regular support, whether livelihood packages help households retain assets through a later shock, and whether a government delivery system changes effects observed under NGO implementation. The World Bank framework offers a real programmatic comparator. The design challenge is to separate the effect of the intervention from who receives it and how a delivery system functions under conflict.

**Anticipatory action and resilience.** The [3R revision](../01_ground-truth-and-research-plan/Ground%20Truth%20-%20PhD%20Research%20Plan%20-%203R%20Revision.md) already identifies forecast-based financing, pre-positioned payments, and preventing distress sales. [Oxford's Bangladesh anticipatory-cash research](https://www.csae.ox.ac.uk/anticipatory-cash-transfers-in-climate-disaster-response) and [WFP's Nepal evaluation](https://www.wfp.org/publications/nepal-anticipatory-action-impact-evaluation) show that timing can be evaluated causally in some contexts. A nexus question is whether the benefit persists into the next season, protects productive assets, or alters reliance on later relief, rather than only whether cash arrived before peak flooding. Transfer to conflict settings would require separate checks of forecastability, payment access, security, and implementation. The unverified “GoDARTS” platform from the transcript should not be used as an anchor.

**Insurance and pre-arranged finance.** Distinguish the forecast, the index's relationship to actual loss, the payout rule, payout delivery, and welfare effects. The user's hypothesis that funders demand stronger predictive evidence is plausible but should be tested by product and funder. Basis risk, false alarms, missed losses, and whose risk is transferred are central, especially with repeated shocks. The Bangladesh insurance replication is a relevant project, but its missing public village linkage limits a proposed spatial validation. An insurance case can therefore test a distinct evidence standard without making all resilience planning an insurance question.

**Displacement, return, and reconstruction.** The [3R revision](../01_ground-truth-and-research-plan/Ground%20Truth%20-%20PhD%20Research%20Plan%20-%203R%20Revision.md) also emphasizes capital replacement and return. External evidence on cash, social protection, or livelihood recovery may fail if people move, land rights change, or services are provided through different institutions. The researcher could examine how the target population and outcome definition change across relief and recovery, and whether interventions protect capabilities in both host and return areas. This is a substantive transportability question, though it may require data and partnerships beyond the current Somalia/Sudan panel.

**Operational evidence at the boundary.** Market functionality, access, and delivery matter because they determine whether an intervention exists in practice. The new [potential data sources register](../06_data-sources-and-handoffs/Potential%20Data%20Sources%20-%20Research-Wide%20Register.md) records JMMI, 3W, IATI, and other leads with access caveats. The research role here need not be optimizing each convoy: operational variables can serve as treatment-fidelity measures, effect modifiers, or veto conditions for borrowing causal evidence. The [JMMI handoff](../06_data-sources-and-handoffs/JMMI%20API%20-%20Somalia%20and%20Sudan%20Panel%20Test%20Handoff.md) proposes a bounded API and join test, not panel integration.

## What the next presentation should claim—and test

The new opening could pose a **prolonged-crisis decision**: after several seasons of drought and displacement, a government, donor, and humanitarian partners must decide whether to continue relief cash, connect households to a regular safety net, add livelihood support, and pre-arrange help for the next shock. They have evidence from elsewhere and partial local information, but implementation and institutions differ. The intellectual question is: *what can be learned from external experience, what must be checked locally, and how can uncertainty be communicated before a multi-year choice?*

This framing keeps the original 2×2 as supporting research infrastructure: evidence discovery, context fusion, evidence mapping, and replicable trials. It removes the acute 72-hour planner from the role of primary user. The practitioner-facing product could be a decision-specific evidence brief, a transferability audit, a comparison of mechanisms and implementation conditions, or a validated forecast/trigger analysis. A live simulator is a much stronger claim and would require demonstrated data linkage, identifiable outcomes, and user value. The agenda can explicitly test whether any of those products changes a decision.

Several tensions should remain visible rather than be resolved rhetorically:

- **Time and reversibility:** Which decisions have enough time for research, and which can be adapted after new information arrives?
- **Comparability:** Does “cash transfer” mean the same treatment under NGO delivery, public social protection, mobile money, and interrupted markets?
- **Outcome horizon:** Does an intervention improve immediate consumption, reduce asset sales, sustain schooling, or support durable recovery? These are not interchangeable effects.
- **Multiple shocks:** When does a source study's hazard, conflict level, or displacement pattern cease to resemble the target setting?
- **Power and ownership:** Who chooses the research question and outcome, whose data are accessible, and whose judgment can override a model or donor metric?
- **Value of evidence:** When does better causal synthesis change program choice, versus local context data or institutional authority being the actual bottleneck?

An important counterargument remains: a transition-phase agenda could become too broad and vague. “Nexus” should be a **case-selection rule**, not a claim that every relief, resilience, and development problem belongs in one dissertation. A coherent empirical contribution would still need a named decision, intervention family, target setting, outcome horizon, and falsifiable comparison. The existing panel and insurance replication offer starting points, but neither alone proves that the whole research bridge can be built. The present note is intended to help choose that tighter question before rewriting the strategy or deck.
