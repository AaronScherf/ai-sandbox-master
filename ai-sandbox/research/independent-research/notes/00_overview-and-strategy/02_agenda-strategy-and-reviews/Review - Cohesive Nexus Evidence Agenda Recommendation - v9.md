## 2. Distance cases, reassessed [I]
- **Bold et al.** concerns an intervention changing when delivery moves from one type of agent to another and scales up, in a similar context. It illustrates **transformation of the intervention** under scale, not transport between contexts. Keep as background on scaling, not as a distance case.
- **Crost et al. (2014 versus 2016)** compares two different interventions. It shows that conflict can produce counterintuitive results but does not compare conflict and non-conflict contexts directly.
- **Egger versus Kenya HSNP 2** is kept, as a **hypothesis-generating pair**, not as a documented transport failure. The reading: if the interventions are comparable, returns in one setting did not hold in another where a measurable factor (for example market integration) differs, so transport **within** a country is not guaranteed. Conditions that are not yet established **[C]**: the two figures measure different quantities (an import-adjusted expenditure multiplier versus income per shilling in four counties), and the Completion Review does not attribute the gap to a contextual factor. To establish comparability, the two definitions and the factor have to be checked.
- **A proposal arising from it [P]:** an insecure region in one country may resemble an insecure region in another more than the rest of its own country. Conflict zones could then learn from each other, using a synthetic-control-style comparison. What "similar" means is the core design question of RQ3.
- **Bedoya et al. (Afghanistan)** is the aspirational case: a programme that succeeded despite the conflict. The question it raises is **what enabled success** and whether better-matched evidence could raise the chance of such cases. **[I]** Treat it as a case to read, not yet as a result; its reported figures are unchecked by me.
- **Overall [I]:** the literature is not uniform. Designing from available evidence is not copy-and-paste. The question is whether conditioning evidence on context, or finding more relevant evidence, can raise the chance of a Bedoya-type outcome.

## 3. The three research questions, restated [O]

**Market-failure framing.** Research attention and evidence are largely non-market goods. Academic, government and nonprofit researchers, and the organizations that set agendas and fund evidence, may allocate attention inefficiently, while planners still need evidence that the research does not supply. The mismatch is mostly one of **relevance**, not price or quantity. Improving relevance, and allocating evidence generation to genuine gaps that cannot be filled more cheaply by adapting evidence from another context, are two sides of the same question. This includes a measure of how unique a context is, to separate a real research gap from an adaptable one.

| RQ | Question | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **RQ1** | How much **discretion** do planners have on a decision, and how do they **value** available evidence (perceived relevance and usefulness)? How can both be measured? | Where evidence could change a choice; where constraints make it unusable, including constraints set by the organization's central office |
| **RQ2** | What do planners' maps and plans **rest on** (context data, implicit knowledge, best practice, standard procedures), and which assumptions do their causal maps, scenarios and log-frames contain, stated or not? Given constraints, how is the best decision chosen, and is there an **information-search problem** (relevant evidence hard to find) or an **overload problem** (much evidence, few strong signals)? | A documented decision-and-evidence map per decision; where evidence is rejected and why |
| **RQ3** | Given where evidence matters and what blocks it, how can the blocks be reduced: **search cost** (automated discovery), **overload** (synthesis), **context irrelevance** (least-distance evidence, conditioning), **specificity** (what the evidence supports: point estimate, bounds, sign, or only uncertainty)? When can a transportability model be applied **without overstating** transfer, in a way that matches the planner's own causal view (the DAG matches the planner's graph)? | Matched evidence and stated prediction types; prospective tests of the predictions |

**Goal and test [O].** More successful interventions: more appropriate tools, more realistic assumptions, blockers anticipated, more realistic or specific targets. "More successful" is assessed by whether the project's predictions and analysis turn out accurate (pre-registered predictions; v5 §5).

## 4. Stakeholders and audience (slide) [P]
Following the reprokit principle that research should be made with an audience in mind, not only as abstract knowledge. Proposed content for one slide, to be edited:

| Stakeholder | What they decide | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| Field planners and programme staff | Design and adaptation | Evidence matched to their decision and context |
| Headquarters and central research offices | Policies that constrain field choices; research agendas | A view of when field officers use the research the office produces, and when its own policies prevent use |
| Evidence-generation funders (for example 3ie, J-PAL, foundations) | Where to fund new studies | Which gaps are genuine and which can be filled by adapting existing evidence |
| Academic and applied researchers | What to study | Where research would be used |
| Donors and governments | Programme funding and rules | Where rules bind and how discretion can be targeted |

Headquarters/field is a second stakeholder level and may matter for partner approach and for the human-subjects framing (open question 2).
