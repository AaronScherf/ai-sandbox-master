# Kenya HSNP Phase 2: check of its use as a worked example (v1)

*Claude, 2026-11-12. A single pass for the Friday talk, not a full desk audit. Sources: the FCDO Business Case (`iati_documents/4857207.odt`) and the Project Completion Review (`50441178.odt`), re-downloaded and searched in the raw `content.xml` with endnote bodies included. **[C]** = checked in the text; **[I]** = my interpretation. Line numbers refer to my extracted text and will not match another extraction; quotes are short.*

## Question
Does the Kenya case show, with documents alone, (a) a real decision among design options, (b) how evidence entered that decision, and (c) a later result that can be compared with what was expected?

## Findings

1. **A real choice exists, but it is about scale, speed and financing [C].** Seven options; Option 3 "scale up the total caseload ... from 69,000 to 100,000 HH by 2014/15" (slow/early). The options do not differ in intervention type.
2. **The stated basis for the choice is financing and targets, not evidence of effect [C].** The Business Case says Option 3 "achieves significant GoK share of the total caseload (54%) while delivering at 2% over the OP targets and providing a substantial scale-up to test the new targeting approach." The preferred option "is recommended on the basis of being the most likely to build GoK financial sustainability."
3. **The evidence section supports the intervention, not the option [C].** After citing a randomised evaluation of HSNP 1 (24 treatment and 24 control sub-locations; 1,434 and 1,433 households) and international reviews, the Business Case concludes the evidence "supports the case for all options that retain and expand CTs ... a sound basis for ... Options 1, 2, 3, 4, 5b and 6." The evidence does not discriminate between the options. **[I]** This is a clean example for RQ1: evidence was plentiful and still did not decide the choice; financing and targets did.
4. **One risk is raised without an evidence citation in the same passage [C, passage only].** The larger scale-up "carries a modest risk that other lower cost interventions may meet the needs of some of the caseload e.g. one-off cash transfers ... may achieve much of the cushioning impact." I did not search for a supporting citation elsewhere. **[I]** This is the closest the document comes to an alternative intervention type, and it is handled in one sentence.
5. **Evaluation was planned [C].** "HSNP2 will have an independent mid and end evaluation"; the Business Case also describes designing an impact evaluation using a regression-discontinuity approach on the targeting score. The Completion Review lists a Local Economy Wide Impact Evaluation among its documents. I did not read those evaluations.
6. **The log-frame is referenced but I did not read its table [C].** The Business Case points to a logframe by system reference ("QUEST REF: 3915054"); the logframe itself was not in the text I searched. Expected-effect indicators therefore remain unchecked.
7. **Results came back as scored outputs [C].** Overall "A - met expectation". The 100,000-household target was reached, then fell to 96,640 in the final year. Timely payment (Output 2.2) scored C: 71% on time in 2016-17 against a 65% milestone, then 33% in 2017-18. Output 2.1 (coverage) scored B. These are delivery results, not effects on households.
8. **Setting [C]:** a drought-prone, non-conflict context (see the earlier probe notes). It cannot test conflict or displacement.

## Verdict for the talk

- **Suitable** as a worked example of RQ1's core point: the available evidence supported the programme, did not separate the options, and financing/targets decided. Say it as an observation about this document trail, not as a general finding.
- **Not suitable** to show how evidence changes an intervention-type choice, or any conflict dynamic. State the limits on the slide.
- **Do not claim** that the options appraisal ignored evidence; the Business Case says the weightings in its tables drove the front-runners, which I did not examine.

## Not checked
The options weighting tables; the logframe; the HSNP evaluation reports; Ethiopia and Somalia documents.
