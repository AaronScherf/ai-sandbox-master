# Abstract: matching research evidence to decisions in prolonged crises (v2)

*Draft 2026-11-12, supersedes [v1](Abstract%20-%20Nexus%20Evidence%20Agenda%20-%20v1.md). Revised to state the market-failure framing and the roles of the three questions. No figures cited; the literature scan behind it is not systematic.*

## Abstract

Programmes in prolonged crises and in the transition from relief to development are designed by planners who work within constraints set by donors, headquarters, budgets and norms, and who choose among options using whatever evidence they can find and trust. Research on what works is produced for a different market. Evidence and research attention are largely non-market goods, and researchers, funders and organizations that set agendas may allocate them inefficiently, so planners still lack evidence they can use while some well-studied interventions receive further study. This project treats that as a mismatch of relevance, more than of price or quantity, and asks three linked questions. **RQ1:** how much discretion do planners have on a given design decision, and how much do they value the evidence available to them? This identifies where new or better-matched evidence could change a choice, and where constraints, including policies set by the organization's own central office, make evidence unusable, so that researchers and evidence-generation funders can direct effort to where it can matter. **RQ2:** what do planners' maps and plans rest on (context data, implicit knowledge, best practice, standard procedures), what assumptions do their causal maps, scenarios and log-frames contain, and where does the choice of the best decision under constraints run into a search problem (relevant evidence is hard to find) or an overload problem (too much evidence, too few signals)? **RQ3:** given where evidence is most useful and what blocks it, can the blocks be reduced, by lowering search cost, synthesizing evidence, finding evidence at the least distance from the planner's context (including across similar crisis settings in different countries), and stating what the evidence supports (a point estimate, bounds, a direction, or only uncertainty), without overstating how far findings transfer? Predictions are pre-registered and tested prospectively. The contribution is a way to measure discretion and the value of evidence for specific decisions, a method for matching existing evidence to context and separating genuine research gaps from gaps that can be filled by adapting evidence from elsewhere, and guidance for funders and central offices on where to generate evidence next.

## Short version (three sentences)

Planners in prolonged crises choose designs under real constraints, and the evidence produced for them is plentiful for some interventions, sparse for others, and often poorly matched to their context. The project measures planner discretion and the value planners place on evidence (RQ1), documents what their plans rest on and where search or overload blocks use (RQ2), and builds ways to make evidence more relevant by conditioning on context and by finding the closest comparable settings (RQ3). It aims to show where better-matched evidence would change decisions, and where a gap is genuine rather than adaptable from elsewhere, so that research effort is allocated where it can be used.
