## 1. What Changed from v1

This second version (v2) incorporates critical corrections, structural rubric refinements, and status re-evaluations following independent spot-checks of primary documents:
1. **Split Criterion 1 (Options in Design):** Split into two distinct criteria:
   - **1a. Options compared:** Assesses whether discrete design options were explicitly formulated and comparatively evaluated (0 = absent, 1 = partial/informal, 2 = rich formal appraisal).
   - **1b. Options differ in kind:** Assesses whether compared options represent fundamentally distinct programmatic modalities or delivery architectures (e.g., cash vs. in-kind food, unconditional grants vs. public works, government agency vs. parallel humanitarian delivery). A score of 0 is assigned if compared options are merely variants of scale, speed, targeting thresholds, or budget/co-financing shares of a single intervention type.

## 2. Summary Scoring Table & Rubric Definitions

### Rubric Scoring Anchors
Scoring is calibrated across eight dimensions (0 = Absent, 1 = Partial, 2 = Rich):
- **1a. Options compared:** 0 = Single design presented without alternatives; 1 = Informal or partial discussion of alternatives; 2 = Formal comparative appraisal of discrete options against a counterfactual.
- **1b. Options differ in kind:** 0 = Options represent only variations in scale, speed, or financing of a single intervention; 1 = Partial comparison of distinct modalities within sub-components; 2 = Formal comparison across fundamentally distinct programmatic designs or delivery mechanisms.
- **1c. Rejection rationale recorded (Anchor):** *The documentation explicitly records why alternative programmatic options, modalities, or delivery mechanisms were rejected based on comparative empirical evidence, cost-effectiveness, or institutional constraints.*
- **2. Evidence-use trace (Anchor):** *The ex-ante design document cites specific external or prior empirical studies with identifiable authors and dates that can be located and opened, and explicitly ties those findings to specific operational design choices.*
- **3. M&E data across life:** 0 = Discontinuous or missing; 1 = Periodic reporting with data gaps; 2 = Continuous, multi-year results framework tracked across regular disclosable supervision reports.
- **4. Closeout evaluation (Anchor):** *The closeout report or completion review explicitly evaluates whether ex-ante design mechanisms and assumptions (e.g., targeting formulas, contingency thresholds, payment systems, or capacity timelines) functioned as predicted and identifies root causes of design divergence.*
- **5. Geographic specificity:** 0 = National aggregate only; 1 = Regional breakdown; 2 = Granular district/woreda allocations, livelihood zone differentiation, or GIS-mapped triggers.
- **6. Support-seeking record:** 0 = Opaque implementation; 1 = High-level partner mentions; 2 = Fully documented commercial contracts, terms of reference, firm names, and expenditure shares.
- **7. Organization status:** 0 = Defunct/inaccessible; 1 = Reorganized with archival gaps; 2 = Fully active institutions maintaining public programmatic archives.

### Structural Effect of Funder Reporting Standards
- **World Bank Investment Project Financing (IPF):** Focuses on technical feasibility, fiduciary safeguards, and cost-benefit/poverty models. Modality choices (e.g., cash vs. food in PSNP 4) are evaluated within technical annexes rather than as an explicit horse-race between alternative programme proposals, unless driven by environmental and social analysis of alternatives.
- **UK DFID/FCDO Business Cases:** Governed by HM Treasury's Five-Case Model, which legally mandates an *Appraisal Case* defining, scoring, and comparing multiple feasible options against a "do-nothing" counterfactual. This institutional rule ensures high scores on Criterion 1a, but as demonstrated below, these options often vary parameters (scale, timeline, budget share) of a single preferred intervention rather than alternatives differing in kind.

### What Kenya HSNP 2 CAN and CANNOT Test for the Agenda
- **What HSNP 2 CAN Test:**
  1. *Parametric Crisis Triggers:* How ex-ante empirical evidence was used to design objective, satellite-based Vegetation Condition Index (VCI) triggers that automatically release emergency funds without political discretion.
  2. *Commercial Delivery Mechanics:* The viability of testing commercial agent banking and biometric smartcards (Equity Bank) as a scalable crisis-response delivery mechanism in underdeveloped remote markets.
  3. *Evidence-to-Policy Translation in Targeting:* How rigorous empirical evaluations of Phase 1 targeting models (OPM/IDS 2011, Coady et al. 2004) directly produced a hybrid Community-Based Targeting + Proxy Means Test (CBT-PMT) policy.
- **What HSNP 2 CANNOT Test:**
  1. *Decision-Making Under Active Conflict:* It cannot test how aid agencies and governments make decisions, monitor programs, or redirect resources during armed conflict or state collapse.
  2. *Displacement and Migrant Vulnerability:* It cannot test evidence use regarding mobile, displaced, or refugee populations, as its roster was restricted to settled pastoralist county residents.
  3. *Compound Multi-Hazard Tradeoffs:* It cannot test programmatic tradeoffs when facing concurrent, non-drought shocks (e.g., conflict combined with hyperinflation or epidemic disease).
  4. *Dislodging Parallel UN Delivery:* Because HSNP was built collaboratively by DFID and the Government of Kenya from Phase 1, it cannot test the institutional friction of dislodging entrenched parallel UN humanitarian appeals.

## 5. Recommendation for the Desk-Based Retrospective Audit

1. For an initial single-case desk audit, **Kenya HSNP 2 (Candidate C)** remains the cleanest starting point because its design-to-closeout documentation is fully bounded, accessible, and structured under a single coherent bilateral standard (DFID Business Case, five Annual Reviews, and a Project Completion Review).
2. While HSNP 2's options appraisal evaluated variants of a single cash design rather than options differing in kind, its explicit evidence section, quantitative targeting indices, and direct attribution to implementing suppliers (DAI, Equity Bank, OPM) provide an unambiguous paper trail.
3. However, because HSNP 2 is a drought-only programme in a stable institutional setting, it cannot test how decision-makers use evidence amidst active armed conflict or complex displacement.
4. Therefore, expanding to a **two-case comparative design** pairing Kenya with **Ethiopia PSNP 4 (Candidate B)** is strongly recommended and well worth the additional effort.
5. This paired design leverages Kenya for its textbook ex-ante options trace and commercial contractor transparency, while using Ethiopia's candid closeout evaluation (ICR6153) and real-world modality comparisons (cash vs. food, credit vs. asset grants) to test how evidence holds up under conflict, macroeconomic shocks, and implementation failure.

If additional comparative candidates are needed to expand beyond the initial desk audit, four operations surfaced during this probe with promising public documentation:
