### Funder Standardization and Institutional Reporting Formats
Two of the three candidates (Somalia and Ethiopia) are World Bank Investment Project Financing (IPF) operations, while Candidate C is funded by the UK Department for International Development (DFID, now FCDO). This structural difference heavily shapes documentation:
- **World Bank IPF documents** (Project Appraisal Documents) follow an operational blueprint focusing on technical feasibility, fiduciary safeguards, and environmental/social frameworks. They assess economic viability (often via cost-benefit, poverty gap models, or fiscal sustainability), but rarely structure design choices as an explicit horse-race between discrete, competing programmatic designs unless mandated by safeguard alternatives.
- **UK DFID/FCDO Business Cases** follow HM Treasury's Five-Case Model (Strategic, Appraisal, Commercial, Financial, Management). The *Appraisal Case* formally mandates the definition, scoring, and economic comparison of multiple feasible options against a "do-nothing" counterfactual. Consequently, UK-funded programmes inherently score higher on Criterion 1 (Options in Design) due to institutional reporting standards rather than superior historical decision-making per se.

#### Rubric Scores and Evidence
1. **Design document with options: 1 (Partial)**. PAD3421 (2019-07-30) outlines the program design and components, but does not present a formal comparative matrix of alternatives considered (e.g., vouchers vs cash, public works vs UCT). It notes that using WFP was "considered as the first option" (p. 86) and that MoLSA explored options for shifting aid (p. 16), but alternatives are not systematically scored or compared.
2. **Evidence-use trace: 1 (Partial)**. Contextual surveys (RVI 2017 remittances, FSNAU 2016, UNFPA 2014) are tied to benefit level (US\$20 based on 2018 MEB) and transfer frequency, but external causal or comparative impact evaluations are cited only generically (p. 25 para 53 mentions Mexico, Brazil, Kenya without citations).
3. **M&E data: 2 (Rich)**. Complete Results Framework with PDO and intermediate indicators (PAD3421 pp. 44–49). Tracked across 10 semi-annual disclosable ISRs from November 2019 to September 2024, reporting cumulative beneficiaries (e.g., 2,107,266 actual beneficiaries against 2,520,000 end target in ISR 10, p. 4).
4. **Closeout evaluation: 1 (Partial)**. No World Bank Implementation Completion and Results Report (ICR) exists yet because P171346 is active through December 31, 2025, and P174065 closed on March 31, 2024 without a disclosed ICR. However, rich independent evaluations exist: Samuel Hall's *Targeting Evaluation* (November 2022, 50 pp.) assesses exclusion errors (76%) and inclusion errors (37%) linking them to CBT village elite dynamics; CGIAR/IFPRI's *Review* (October 2024, 90 pp.) assesses delivery against design pathways.
5. **Geographic specificity: 2 (Rich)**. Explicit sub-national allocation formulas across 5 Federal Member States (Galmudug, Hirshabelle, Jubaland, Puntland, South West) and Somaliland, targeting rural districts with high distress while deliberately excluding Banadir/Mogadishu (PAD3421 p. 56 fn 39; mapped in Annex 7 p. 90).
6. **Support-seeking record: 2 (Rich)**. World Bank contract data API and project papers document 89 contracts under P171346, identifying firms (Development Pathways, Raagsan, Transparency Solutions, CCORD, Zamid) and major UN agency output agreements (WFP, UNICEF).
7. **Organization status: 2 (Rich)**. World Bank, MoLSA, WFP, and UNICEF are fully active and implementing SNHCP Third Additional Financing (P181469) through 2025+.

#### Hindsight-Bias Risk Note
Because SNHCP was designed in mid-2019 prior to the 2020 locust invasion, COVID-19 pandemic, and 2021–2023 mega-drought, the appraisal baseline relies strictly on pre-2019 data (2014 PESS, 2016/17 drought reviews, 2018 MEB). Subsequent scale-up papers (PAD3953, PAD4513, PAD5570) reveal severe post-hoc adaptation (expanding caseload from 200k to over 400k households and shifting from manual biometric distribution sites to direct mobile money transfers due to COVID-19). An auditor must isolate the 2019 PAD3421 parameters to avoid attributing 2022–2024 emergency pivots to ex-ante design intent.

#### Hindsight-Bias Risk Note
PSNP 4 offers an unusually transparent view into design-time assumptions versus ex-post reality. PAD1022 assumed a stable macroeconomic environment and no major catastrophic shocks. During implementation, the 2015/16 El Niño drought, the COVID-19 pandemic, and violent conflict in northern Ethiopia (Tigray) severely disrupted implementation. The ICR (ICR6153 para 94) notes that supervision ISRs downplayed these strains for years before the completion evaluation candidly acknowledged systems design failures. A retrospective audit must bound design knowledge to September 2014 (PAD1022).

#### External Evidence Citations at Design (Business Case 4857207)
1. **OPM & IDS (2011), *HSNP Targeting Effectiveness Evaluation Report*** (cited paras 518, 528): Rigorously evaluated the 3 targeting mechanisms piloted in Phase 1 (Community-Based Targeting [CBT], Social Pension, and Dependency Ratio). Beneficiary households in CBT areas were 50% more likely to be poor than non-beneficiaries, but showed substantial inclusion/exclusion errors.
2. **Coady, Grosh, & Hoddinott (2004), *Targeting of Transfers in Developing Countries: Review of Lessons and Experience* (World Bank & IFPRI)** (cited para 530): Used to compute Coady-Grosh-Hoddinott (CGH) targeting indices for CBT (1.21), Social Pension (1.17), Dependency Ratio (1.15), and simulated Proxy Means Testing (PMT) (1.47).
   - *Tied to design choice:* Directly justified abandoning single-method targeting in favour of a **hybrid CBT + PMT targeting mechanism** (para 533) where community committees identify candidates whose poverty status is verified via PMT scores.
3. **Bold, Porteous, & Rotman / CGAP (2012), *Social Cash Transfers and Financial Inclusion*** (cited para 625): Directly tied to contracting a commercial private sector bank (Equity Bank) to open fully functional bank accounts with biometric smartcards rather than closed-loop voucher systems.
4. **IEG / World Bank (2011), *Social Safety Nets: An Evaluation of World Bank Support, 2000–2010*** (cited para 509): Cites global findings that long-term non-lending technical assistance embedded in government agencies is essential for building sustainable national safety net institutions.
5. **National Audit Office (NAO 2011) & DFID (2011) Payment Technology Studies** (cited para 575): Tied to setting fee caps and security protocols for point-of-sale agent banking in pastoralist areas.

DAI fielded a 10-person resident technical assistance team embedded directly in the National Drought Management Authority (NDMA) supporting MIS, FM, operations, and policy (PCR paras 35, 511; DAI accounted for 6.5% of total programme expenditure).

- **Rights and Grievance Accountability Partner:** **HelpAge International**, funded via an accountable grant to manage civil society mobilization and local grievance redress.

#### Rubric Scores and Evidence
1. **Design document with options: 2 (Rich)**. Business Case Appraisal Case Section A explicitly defines and compares **seven feasible options** (Option 1 Fast/Early to Option 6 Flat, plus Counterfactual) varying caseload scale (69k to 130k HH), speed of scale-up, and GoK funding share (40% to 54%). Each option is scored across a multi-criteria weighting matrix (paras 273–355, 699–701, 903–905) explicitly justifying Option 3.
2. **Evidence-use trace: 2 (Rich)**. Appraisal Case Section B (paras 495–650) evaluates evidence across 4 distinct pillars. The Coady-Grosh-Hoddinott (2004) and OPM/IDS (2011) targeting studies are explicitly cited to justify the hybrid CBT-PMT model (paras 528–533), and CGAP (2012) evidence justifies commercial bank accounts.
3. **M&E data: 2 (Rich)**. Full Logical Framework (doc 5781337) and five published Annual Reviews (2014, 2015, 2016, 2017, 2019) with annual performance scores (B, A+, A, A, A, A), continuous bi-monthly operational monitoring reports by OPM/PILU, and logframe milestone tracking.

Project Completion Review (50441178, August 2019, 685 paras) provides a thorough ex-post assessment of £119.85m spend against logframe targets, evaluates supplier performance (DAI, Equity Bank, OPM), and traces operational bottlenecks (e.g. agent liquidity during drought scale-ups) back to design assumptions.
5. **Geographic specificity: 2 (Rich)**. Concentrated in 4 arid northern counties: Turkana, Marsabit, Mandera, and Wajir. Scalable emergency cash transfers are triggered at sub-county level based on 1 km x 1 km satellite Vegetation Condition Index (VCI) pixels (PCR para 35, Business Case para 6).

#### Hindsight-Bias Risk Note
Because HSNP 2 was designed in 2013 following the 2011 Horn of Africa drought, the design explicitly intended to build an automated, objective shock-response trigger to remove political delay. The 2016 Process Review and 2019 PCR document that while the VCI satellite triggers functioned with near-perfect technical fidelity, the downstream private agent banking network experienced massive cash liquidity bottlenecks when trying to disburse emergency payments to 200,000 pastoralists in remote deserts within two weeks. A retrospective audit can cleanly compare the 2013 ex-ante design assumptions (Business Case 4857207) with the observed operational bottlenecks in 2015/16.

## 3. Recommendation for the Desk-Based Retrospective Audit

### Primary Recommendation: **Candidate C (Kenya HSNP 2)** is the strongest and cleanest foundation for the desk audit.
- **Why it qualifies best:** HSNP 2 is the only candidate that achieves a **maximum score of 14/14**. Its design document (DFID Business Case 4857207) contains an explicit, text-book options appraisal comparing 7 discrete options with weighted criteria and cost-efficiency benchmarks, alongside an explicit evidence-use section that directly links academic literature (Coady et al. 2004, CGAP 2012) to design choices. Its entire lifecycle is bounded and completed (2013–2019), with annual reviews and an exhaustive Project Completion Review on file. Furthermore, the commercial and technical support record is exceptionally clean: DAI (PILU), Equity Bank (payment provider), and OPM (evaluation) are clearly demarcated.

### Secondary Alternative: **Candidate B (Ethiopia PSNP 4)** is a viable, high-volume secondary choice (Score: 13/14).
- **Fit and Caveats:** PSNP 4 possesses an extraordinarily rich evidence trace (linking IFPRI panel surveys, CGAP pilots, and ODI gender studies directly to design parameters) and an outstanding closeout evaluation (ICR6153 and IEG Review ICRR0023858), where World Bank staff candidly dissect why M&E and systems targets failed. Its primary drawback for the PhD desk audit is that PAD1022 does not contain an ex-ante options appraisal matrix comparing competing alternative architectures, making it harder to test which alternative options the designers formally rejected at appraisal.

### Candidate A (Somalia Baxnaano Sequence) is **Premature** for a Design-to-Closeout Audit (Score: 11/14).
- **Fit and Caveats:** While Somalia matches Aaron's existing geographic context panel and demonstrates a compelling transition from humanitarian cash to a national safety net, it cannot support a full design-to-closeout audit today because its core parent project (P171346) is still active through December 31, 2025, and lacks an official Bank Implementation Completion and Results Report. It is better suited as a prospective or contemporary case study than a closed retrospective audit.
