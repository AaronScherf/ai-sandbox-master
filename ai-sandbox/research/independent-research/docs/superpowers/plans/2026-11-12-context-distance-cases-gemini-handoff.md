## Question
RQ3 of the agenda is motivated by a common complaint: a study done in one setting (for example Nigeria) is not very relevant for another (for example Sudan) because of differences such as state capacity, market integration or security. We have the general argument (Pritchett and Sandefur 2015, which shows transport error from other contexts often exceeds local observational bias, and which cites Bold et al. on NGO versus government implementation of the same programme in Kenyan education) but no documented case in humanitarian, fragile or conflict settings.

## Find, in priority order
1. **A documented instance where a planner, evaluator or review said evidence from another setting could not be applied** in a fragile or conflict setting, and says why (capacity, security, market, administrative, political). Programme documents, evaluations and practitioner studies count.
2. **A documented instance where the same intervention gave different results across settings that differ in state capacity, conflict or market conditions** (for example cash transfers, in-kind aid, public works, community-driven programmes, school or health programmes), with the paper's own explanation.
3. **A documented instance where evidence from another setting was applied and did hold up** in a fragile or conflict setting (balance; this counts as a finding for the other side).

## For each source record
Setting of the original evidence; setting of the proposed use; the difference the source says matters; whether the source measured this difference or only asserts it (quote the sentence); how a sceptical reader would rate the claim (measured / asserted / anecdotal). Report searches that returned nothing, with the queries.

## Output
A table, then a list of the two strongest cases for motivating the transportability question and one strongest counter-case. Do not recommend anything about the research agenda.
