# Handoff to Gemini: adversarial scan of the nexus evidence-use finding (time-boxed)

2026-11-12, from Claude. **For a fresh agent session: do not read other notes in the `notes/00_overview-and-strategy` folder (especially anything named "Nexus Evidence Use Scan") before you finish. Independence from earlier work is the point.**

A research agenda assumes that planners of programmes in protracted crises have some room to choose designs, but lack evidence that is relevant to their context or specific enough to act on. Earlier work searching for support may reflect how it searched or read, not only what exists. This task tries to **disprove** that assumption. Finding little, or only weak claims, is a valid and useful result. Do not stretch to find support for either side.

The hypotheses to sort sources into: **A** little demand for evidence beyond operational logistics; **B** abundant evidence supplied but not used; **C** evidence used broadly and effectively; **D** planners make do with what they have and say they would do better with more relevant, specific or usable evidence; **E** existing evidence is adequate and other constraints bind; **F** insufficient evidence either way.

Same rules as before: nothing from memory; every row needs a URL you opened, a short verbatim quote (under 25 words), page or section, and a tag (FULL-TEXT-READ, ABSTRACT-READ, LANDING-PAGE-ONLY). No edits to existing notes; one new file, `Novelty Check - Nexus Evidence Use Adversarial Scan - v1.md`; no commits to main. Time-box: about 15 sources or 90 minutes.

## Step 1: write the search protocol BEFORE reading anything
List the exact queries and sources you will use, per target below, and the inclusion rule (setting, date, document type). Save it as the first section of the output. After searching, record for each query the number of hits you looked at and how many you kept. Report empty or weak searches as results.

## Step 2: search for sources that would fit the other states
Setting filter: conflict or complex crises (for example Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Yemen, DRC, northeast Nigeria, Syria, Afghanistan); note any non-conflict cases separately.
- **C (evidence used broadly and effectively):** programme documents, evaluations or reviews showing that design or adaptation decisions in such settings were explicitly shaped by impact evaluations or synthesised evidence, with the decision changed as a result. Look for adaptive or learning-focused programmes, evidence-to-policy case studies and donor reviews that report uptake.
- **Adequate supply (not D):** sources in which planners or reviewers say existing evidence was sufficient, relevant and timely for the design choice.
- **A (little demand beyond logistics):** sources showing planners deliberately do not want or need effect evidence for design (for example because choices are fixed by mandate, donor rules or logistics).
- **Evidence travels:** research showing that effects measured in other (non-crisis or different) settings predicted outcomes well in conflict or fragile settings, or that cross-site heterogeneity is small for the intervention in question (for example cash or in-kind transfers across contexts). Include reviews of effect heterogeneity.
- **Constraints, not evidence, bind:** sources arguing that planners already know what works and the barrier is money, access or politics.

## Step 3: read, and separate searching from reading
For each source record: setting (conflict or complex crisis versus other), evidence type, what it is used for, and which hypothesis it supports. Give the strongest quote **against** the working hypothesis and, if any, for it. Add a column "would a sceptical reader accept this reading? (yes / contestable)" and say why when contestable.

## Output
1. The protocol and the per-query hit counts.
2. A table of kept sources as above.
3. Counts of sources per hypothesis (A to F) and per setting type.
4. What you could not find. Do not recommend whether the research questions are worth pursuing.
