## Why this scan
The question is whether the research questions are worth asking, not whether the field exists. Prior work shows evidence rarely drives immediate-response decisions (ALNAP, Campbell and Knox Clarke 2019). Nobody has shown how planners of **longer-horizon design and adaptation in protracted crises** use or lack evidence. Hypotheses to sort sources into (not exhaustive; add "insufficient evidence" and "other" freely):
- **A. Little demand beyond operational logistics.**
- **B. Abundant evidence supplied, but not used.**
- **C. Evidence used broadly and effectively.**
- **D. Planners make do with what they have, and say they would do better with evidence that is more relevant to their context, more specific (treatment effects, comparisons of intervention types), or less technical and quicker to use.**

## What to find (aim for a mix; do not stop at the nexus-policy literature)
1. **Programme design and appraisal documents** for programmes in conflict or complex-crisis settings (for example World Bank project appraisal documents and completion reports, FCDO business cases and annual reviews, EU or ECHO programme documents, UN agency programme documents), in countries such as Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Yemen, DRC, northeast Nigeria, Syria or Afghanistan. Look at how they cite evidence for design choices.
2. **Evaluations or reviews of nexus, resilience, social-protection-in-crisis or adaptive programmes** that comment on how evidence was or was not used in design and adaptation.
3. **Studies or surveys of practitioners and planners** (not researchers) about evidence needs and use in protracted crises or nexus programming. Prefer sources that ask planners what they want.

## For each source record
- **Setting:** conflict or complex crisis (name it) versus disaster, drought or otherwise peaceful poor country. Be explicit; this distinction matters.
- **Evidence type cited or wanted:** impact evaluation or RCT; programme evaluation; best-practice or lessons-learned guidance; needs or vulnerability assessment; expert or colleague opinion; other. Note any stated quality standard (for example strength-of-evidence ratings).
- **What it is used for:** a mandatory design step; choosing between options; active adaptation of a running programme; justification after the fact; advocacy; other.
- **Supply signals:** is evidence described as abundant, scarce, or not relevant to the context?
- **Demand signals:** what do planners say they need (context-specific, specific treatment effects, comparison of intervention types, plain language, speed) or say they lack (time, skills, access)?
- **Hypothesis fit:** A / B / C / D / insufficient / other, with one sentence of reasoning.

## Output
One table with the columns above, then: (1) a count of sources per hypothesis and per setting type; (2) the three strongest quotes for and against "planners would benefit from more tailored or more specific evidence"; (3) what the scan could not cover. Do not recommend whether the research questions are worth pursuing; Aaron and Claude will judge that.
