CTL’s Teaching Development Program (TDP) is a comprehensive new service that will allow Columbia doctoral students to cultivate, document, and reflect on teaching development across the arc of their graduate school career. Graduate students can participate in the TDP program on either of two tracks: Foundational Track and Advanced Track.

CTL runs a number of fellowships for graduate students that offer stipends and unique, structured professional development experience. Additional paid professional development opportunities for graduate students entail training and support to offer CTL teaching support services to peers. Priority for these opportunities is given to applicants who are enrolled in the Teaching Development Program, have completed the Essentials of Teaching and Learning series, and/or have completed a CTL seminar for graduate students.

## Teaching Development Program (TDP)

### Structure and pace

The TDP is a two-track program. When you join, you start on the Foundational Track. After completing the majority of its requirements, you can decide whether to exit out of the program with Foundational Track certification, or shift onto the Advanced Track.

The pace of moving through the TDP is up to you; the Foundational Track can be completed in as little as one well-organized semester; completion of the Advanced Track can be completed in as little as an additional year. A track can be completed for credit anytime before graduation.

## Foundational Track

The Teaching Development Program’s Foundational Track introduces you to fundamental concepts and practices in student-centered teaching. It helps to deepen the experience of TAships, connecting early instructional experience to proven and sustainable pedagogical practices.

### Foundational Track objectives

Graduate students who complete the Foundational Track are able to:

- Describe and implement pedagogical practices that draw on evidence-based educational research
- Develop learning goals and aligned assessment strategies that are clear and measurable
- Receive and apply peer-to-peer feedback on teaching practice
- Articulate and reflect on a student-centered approach to teaching

### Foundational Track requirements

To fulfill requirements, attend a CTL program or service – you can find offerings that satisfy requirements on the [TDP Event Finder](https://tdp.ctl.columbia.edu/events/) – and then post a reflection on it in the [TDP course site](https://courseworks2.columbia.edu/courses/58549). We provide [guidance for these reflections](https://tdp.ctl.columbia.edu/progress/reflectionguidelines/), and you will receive feedback on early submissions to make sure you’re on track. For more information about TDP requirements, see [TDP FAQs](https://tdp.ctl.columbia.edu/about/faqs/), visit [office hours](https://ctl.columbia.edu/graduate-instructors/programs-for-graduate-students/office-hours/), or contact [CTLgrads@columbia.edu](mailto:CTLgrads@columbia.edu).

## Advanced Track

If you wish to develop advanced teaching practices and engage more deeply with a community of peers, we invite you to shift onto the TDP’s Advanced Track once you have completed a majority of Foundational Track requirements. The Advanced Track puts special emphasis on transferable professional skills that are cultivated through teaching practices and culminates in the development of a digital teaching portfolio.

### Advanced Track objectives

In addition to the Foundational Track objectives above, graduate students who complete the Advanced Track are able to:

- Define and pursue inquiry-based development as a teacher
- Create a portfolio of teaching artifacts, strategies, and outcomes
- Demonstrate organized, engaging, and distinctive communication practices that can extend into future professional settings

This new fellowship is for graduate students with advanced interest in the impact of AI on teaching and learning, through cultivation of AI critical literacy, and exploration of models for effectively teaching with or around this technology.

# Teaching and AI Fellowship

Fast-moving developments in Generative AI have opened up innumerable new opportunities and challenges for instructors. This fellowship for Columbia University doctoral students is dedicated to exploring pathways for effective teaching in a world where this technology is readily available to instructors and students.

Supported by CTL, TAIFs explore pathways for effective teaching in a world where AI is readily available to instructors and students. In the fall, TAIFs participate in a seminar run by CTL on critical AI literacy. In the spring, some TAIFs develop models for using AI to facilitate learning activities, while others run learning communities for peers, discussing approaches to teaching that are responsive (and possibly even resistant) to AI technology.

This fellowship is open to Columbia graduate students in **[good standing](https://www.gsas.columbia.edu/content/good-standing)** who are in years 2 – 7 in a Columbia University doctoral program as of Fall 2026, or are MFA students in the School of the Arts.

### About the TAIF application

Applications for 2026-27 Teaching and AI Fellows were accepted through **June 15, 2026**. If you have questions about the TAIF fellowship, please reach out to CTLgrads@columbia.edu.
## Seminars

### Human Learning, Artificial Intelligence: A Critical AI Literacy Seminar

This six-week seminar for graduate students convenes a community of peers with a range of views of generative AI technology, and curiosity about how — or whether — to incorporate it into classes. Its focus will be on connecting established evidence about how students learn to emerging research on how AI impacts that learning. Engaging in dialogue and experimentation, participants will develop a stance on the place of  AI in higher education that is evidence-informed and nuanced.

In Fall 2026, this seminar will **start during the week of October 15** (specific times TBA). Applications will be accepted from September 18 - 28.
