# Microeconomics Part 2 study guides: planning document (v1, DRAFT)

Dated 2026-11-26. Not final: 2024 Recitations 3–5 and the December review session are still being transcribed.

## Intent
Review all of Microeconomic Analysis I, Part 2 (Salanié, Fall 2026), including the intermediate-micro background it assumes. The reader has not studied micro in several years, so assume little or no background. There is no gap diagnostic: cover everything and let the reader skim.

**Goals:** self-contained guides, one per topic block, that teach the background first and then the course result.
**Non-goals:** transcribing handwritten notes; mapping the scratch notes made for autoframing work.
**Anti-goals:** solving open graded work (Problem Set 4, due Dec 4); repeating source typos as fact; relying on a transcription for a key formula without checking it.
**Dates:** final exam between Dec 17 and 23.

## The guides

Lecture page numbers (pp.) refer to this year's slide PDFs. "MWG" is Mas-Colell, Whinston and Green; "JR" is Jehle and Reny.

### Guide 0. Toolkit (prerequisites)
- **Math:** partial derivatives, Lagrangians, Kuhn–Tucker corner conditions, convex sets, concave and quasiconcave functions.
- **Consumers:** preferences, utility, indifference curves, MRS, budget constraints, Marshallian demand, Cobb–Douglas and quasilinear cases, income and substitution effects.
- **Producers:** production functions and sets, MRTS and MRT, returns to scale, fixed costs, cost and profit maximization.
- **Markets:** supply and demand, price taking, surplus, partial vs. general equilibrium, the Edgeworth box.
- **Risk and time:** expected utility, risk aversion, CARA and CRRA, discounting.
- **Sources:** JR ch. 1–4, §2.4, appendices A1–A2; 2024 Recitation 1 (Edgeworth box).

### Guide 1. Economy and technology
- **Topics:** general vs. partial equilibrium, the economy's primitives, production plans and sets, assumptions on production sets, aggregation, allocations and feasibility.
- **Course sources:** Lecture 1; Lecture 2 (opening, both versions); Problem Set 1 and TA solutions.
- **Textbooks:** MWG 5.B, 5.E, 15.A, 15.C; JR ch. 3 and §5.3.
- **Assumes:** production functions, isoquants, returns to scale, fixed costs, convexity, constrained maximization.

### Guide 2. Pareto optimality
- **Topics:** Pareto optimum, utility possibility set, welfare weights; Robinson Crusoe, pure exchange and quasilinear examples; differential conditions in smooth convex economies; MRS = MRT; the "nonzero" restriction and corners; production efficiency.
- **Course sources:** Lecture 2 (from "Optimum"), Lecture 3, Lecture 4 pp. 1–28; Problem Set 2 and TA solutions.
- **Supplements:** 2024 Recitations 1–2; "Inside the Edgeworth Box" sets (2024 PS3, 2025 PS3) and solutions.
- **Textbooks:** MWG 15.B, 15.D, 16.E–F, 22.C; JR §5.1.
- **Assumes:** Edgeworth box, MRS and MRT, Lagrangians and Kuhn–Tucker, Cobb–Douglas and quasilinear utility, surplus.

### Guide 3. Time and risk
- **Topics:** information and decisions, feasible plans, dated contingent commodities, ex ante vs. ex post optimality, efficient risk sharing, the mutuality principle, different beliefs.
- **Course sources:** Lecture 4 pp. 29–69, Lecture 5 pp. 2–7; Problem Set 3 and TA solutions.
- **Supplements:** 2024 Recitation 2; 2025 PS1 part II (CARA risk sharing).
- **Textbooks:** MWG 6.B–C, 19.B, 20.B; JR §2.4, §5.4.
- **Assumes:** expected utility, risk aversion, CARA, conditional expectation, discounting.

### Guide 4. Equilibrium and welfare
- **Topics:** Walrasian equilibrium, excess demand and its properties, the core, first welfare theorem, equilibrium and core with production, second welfare theorem (both proofs, separating hyperplane, choosing endowments).
- **Course sources:** Lecture 5 pp. 8–9; Lecture 6 pp. 7–8; Lecture 7 pp. 8–17; Lecture 8 pp. 2–9. Problem Set 4: teach its tools only.
- **Supplements:** 2024 Recitation 2; homothetic-preference and decentralization exercises (2024 PS4, 2025 PS4).
- **Textbooks:** MWG 15.B, 16.B–D, 18.B; JR §5.1–5.3, §5.5.
- **Assumes:** Marshallian demand, homogeneity, Walras' law, profit maximization, relative prices.

### Guide 5. Existence of equilibrium
- **Topics:** two-good case, a first existence theorem, more than two goods, fixed points in a convex exchange economy, unbounded demand, correspondences.
- **Course sources:** Lecture 6 pp. 3–6 and 9–11; Lecture 7 pp. 2–7.
- **Textbooks:** MWG 17.B–C and Mathematical Appendix; Ok, *Real Analysis* (fixed points, correspondences).
- **Assumes:** continuity, compactness, convexity, correspondences, upper hemicontinuity.

### Guide 6. Further equilibrium theory
- **Topics:** Sonnenschein–Mantel–Debreu, stability and tâtonnement, gross substitutes and uniqueness, complete and incomplete asset markets, Radner equilibrium.
- **Course sources:** Lecture 8 pp. 10–22; 2024 final part I.
- **Supplements:** 2025 PS5; 2025 Lecture 9 pp. 2–8; 2017 final (incomplete markets).
- **Textbooks:** MWG 17.E–H, 19.C–F.
- **Assumes:** income and substitution effects, Slutsky equation, comparative statics, matrix rank, assets.

### Guide 7. Public goods and externalities
Not yet lectured this year; built on the 2025 lectures and the 2026 syllabus.
- **Topics:** why the first welfare theorem fails; public goods and Bowen–Lindahl–Samuelson conditions; Lindahl prices and free-riding; real vs. pecuniary externalities; social marginal cost and marginal social damage; permits and Pigouvian taxes; abatement and subsidies; local consumption externalities and person-specific taxes; the Coase theorem and its limits.
- **Course sources:** 2025 Lecture 9 pp. 9–14, Lecture 10, Lecture 11 pp. 2–7. Lindahl prices are on the syllabus but not those slides: use MWG.
- **Supplements:** 2024 final part II; 2016 final (public goods); 2025 PS1 part I; 2024 Recitations 4–5 (pending).
- **Textbooks:** MWG 11.A–D.
- **Assumes:** surplus, marginal cost and benefit, Guides 2 and 4.

### Guide 8. Social choice
Not yet lectured this year.
- **Topics:** majority rule and Condorcet cycles, Borda voting, dictatorship; Arrow's conditions, theorem and proof; single-peaked preferences and the median voter; when majority rule works (Dasgupta–Maskin); individual rights; comparable utilities and Rawls's difference principle.
- **Course sources:** 2025 Lecture 11 pp. 8–13, Lecture 12.
- **Supplements:** 2024 Recitation 5 (pending).
- **Textbooks:** MWG 21.A–E; JR §6.1–6.4.
- **Assumes:** preference orderings and proof technique; no calculus or prices.

## Source rules
1. Authority: this year's slides and TA solutions, then prior-year course material, then textbooks for background. Where a textbook and the course differ in notation or assumptions, follow the course and note the difference.
2. The solution files directly in `problem_sets/` are the student's own work. Use TA solutions in `problem_sets/ta_solutions/` as ground truth.
3. Slides and solutions contain typos. Correct them openly, naming the source page.
4. At least one prior-year solution file has a heading that does not match its file name. Match solutions to problems by content.
5. Transcriptions: text under `academic_notes/microecon/part_2/` (`class_2024/`, `class_2025/` for prior years). This year's transcriptions passed a page-by-page word comparison with their PDFs (Problem Set 1 was not compared; "Handwritten note" blocks in the Lecture 2 copy are student annotations). Some prior-year transcriptions were first fabricated and have been redone. The comparison does not catch wrong subscripts, so check key formulas against the page image.
6. MWG was reconverted on 2026-11-26 with no blank pages. Its chapter files are under `academic_notes/microecon/part_1/textbooks/processed_outputs/`; JR's are under `part_2/textbooks/processed_outputs/`.

## Approaches to generation
- **A. One drafting session per guide, from a written handoff (recommended).** Each session loads only that guide's sources; a separate check compares formulas and theorem statements with the sources. Costs one handoff per guide. Uses the Claude subscription, not paid API calls.
- **B. The existing study-guide pipeline (`agent/study_guide/`).** Reuses its tooling. It was built around problem-set guides and has not been checked for topic reviews.
- **C. Draft everything in the planning session.** No handoffs, but nine guides would not fit one context well.

The owner asked for handoffs, which points to A; the choice is not yet confirmed.
