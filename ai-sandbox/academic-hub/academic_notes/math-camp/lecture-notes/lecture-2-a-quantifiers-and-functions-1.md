Let's summarize the key points from the video:

1. **Commutative Property for Real Numbers**: The statement \( x \cdot y = y \cdot x \) is true for all real numbers \( x \) and \( y \). This is a property of the commutative operation of multiplication in \(\mathbb{R}^n\).

2. **Counterexample for Matrix Multiplication**: The statement \( ab = ba \) is not true for all \( n \times n \) matrices \( a \) and \( b \). A counterexample can be constructed by choosing specific matrices where the order of multiplication results in different outcomes.

3. **Universal and Existential Quantifiers**: 
   - **Universal Quantifier**: A statement like "for every \( a \) and \( b \) in the set of \( n \times n \) matrices, \( ab = ba \)" is universally quantified.
   - **Existential Quantifier**: A statement like "there exists \( a \) and \( b \) in the set of \( n \times n \) matrices such that \( ab \neq ba \)" is existentially quantified.

4. **Variables in Quantifiers**: The variables in quantifiers can be any mathematical objects, such as real numbers, matrices, sets, etc. For example, \( x \) is a dummy variable, \( n \) is often used for natural numbers, and \( a \) and \( b \) are used for matrices.

To provide a concrete counterexample for matrix multiplication, consider the following matrices:
\[ a = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \]
\[ b = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \]

Now, compute both \( ab \) and \( ba \):
\[ ab = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \]
\[ ba = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \]

Since \( ab \neq ba \), this provides a counterexample showing that matrix multiplication is not commutative.
